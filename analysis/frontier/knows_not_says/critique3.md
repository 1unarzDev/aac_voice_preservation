# knows_not_says, round 3 critique (adversarial: methods/statistics + novelty)

Verdict: valid = true as a PROVISIONAL, honestly labelled negative result. No fatal integrity breach found. Several major issues would stop it passing a journal review as written. I re-ran `src/frontier_knows_not_says_test.py` (all tests pass) and re-read r1/r2/holm CSVs, the validity key and code. I did not re-run the GPU arm.

## Fatal issues
None. The amendments (A18-A25) predate the single full run, the dry run is disclosed, and the Holm family was declared before the run. A1(c) failure is reported as a failure.

## Major issues
1. R2 is measured with a score that cannot resolve the event. Sampled share uses K = 16 on an event with p of about 0.5% to 1.6%. 97% (qwen) and 87% (llama) of units tie at 0, so "the model's own distribution carries no per-input signal" is partly a measurement-resolution artefact, not evidence about the model. The ask policy at 10/100 is then random-ask inside a huge tied block. A fair "knows" test needs a continuous score that needs no sampling: the analytic probability mass on negation openers at early positions (top-20 logprobs were already stored), forced-decoding scores of a negated rewrite, or K of 256 or more on the 107 positives plus a matched negative draw. The pair-score arm is the only continuous score and it is run on 164 pairs, not on the R2 units. Until then "R2 fails" should read "K = 16 sampled share is not informative", and the title and the A/B/C story overstate.
2. The "semantic-entropy analogue" and "closest prior method" benchmarks are weak and mislabelled. Semantic entropy clusters meaning across all sampled variants; here the scalar is the negated fraction, which is the very quantity R2 tested. "Semantic entropy does not help" is not established. Also missing from the literature: semantic calibration via sampling, "Trained on Tokens, Calibrated on Concepts" (arXiv 2511.04869, ICLR 2026, seen at search-snippet level only; it is the nearest published frame for the R1 result), and Hou et al. 2024 clarification ensembling, which a reader will expect (I only saw it cited in an ACL 2025 survey snippet). The cited arXiv 2605.25284 ("Knowing but Not Showing", Su and Cardie, May 2026) is verified and is about ambiguity-recognition versus clarifying questions in QA. It is adjacent, not the same, so novelty is plausible but must be argued explicitly against 2511.04869.
3. The R1 target is an artefact of how E-cap4 is built. cap4 is the POS-cap4 keyword reduction, which drops negation words in 540/540 units, so the 10.8% "source posterior" is essentially the corpus rate of negated messages, not a human omission rate (E-human gives 2.6%). Calling the model "overconfident, ratio 0.05" compares the model's conditional behaviour to a marginal base rate under a synthetic filter. Marginal calibration (aggregate share = base rate) is also not what a Bayes-correct per-input predictor must satisfy, since the keywords carry information (lexical AUROC 0.64, E-human 0.75). The plan's own rule says disagreement between datasets means no headline; llama E-human is inconclusive (ratio 0.71 [0.46, 1.12]) yet section 2's headline paragraph states both models as overconfident from cap4 alone. Make the headline qwen-only or "cap4 stress test only".
4. The detector reference convention and sensitivity. (a) The reference counted uncertainty wording and many real clause negations as "not negated": among the 13 source false positives are "no, i won't, i like tis channel", "No, I can't, I have to go somewhere", "milk no sugar", "i can understand you, i just can't talk". Several of these are genuine negations, so PPV 0.74 is partly a codebook choice of the machine judges, not detector error; "I don't know" to "I know" is also a real AAC reversal. The reported PPV and the corrected posterior (9.6%) therefore depend on a convention nobody human has confirmed. (b) NPV = 300/300 comes from 300 random detector-negative outputs at 0.4% to 1.8% prevalence; the Wilson upper bound on the false-negative rate (1.3%) is as large as the detected true-negative prevalence, so the "derived sensitivity 1.00" has a lower bound near 0.2 (qwen) to 0.5 (llama). The corrected-ratio bootstrap does propagate Beta(npv) so classes hold, but the prose "no false negative among 650" and "sensitivity 1.00" should not be stated as findings. (c) Round3 says the arbiter handled 7 disputes (I decided them, from text only); ANNOTATION_REQUEST.md still says 3. Fix.
5. Reversal and recovery claims are simulations that assume asking resolves to the source label, and the headline AAC improvement is by construction. The chip/plain-word rows are oracle-triggered (taps = positives, 107.7 per 1,000), so "104 to about 1 reversal per 1,000" is a tautology minus the honour-rate residual, not a result about a deployable system. Section 7 (iii) places it beside real model-triggered policies without the hedge in the same sentence's numerator. The realistic result is the model-side one: 20-40% of reversals removed at 100-250 extra taps (the lexical ask at 10/100 removes only 80.6 of 104, i.e. about 23%, with a supervised classifier trained on the same source labels). Report the user-needs-to-know-when-to-cue cost explicitly, or add a non-oracle user model.
6. Stop-rule and gating wording. Section 7 cancels Stages 3-4 "by the pre-registered stop rule (R1 overconfident, R2 negative, R3 not discriminating against the model-free baseline)". The PLAN stop rule says "R1 and R2 both negative for all models and R3 fails"; R3 as declared has no lexical comparator, and llama passed R3-a and R3-b (E-cap4). The decision (R3 fails by the queries-per-catch of 20 or fewer at recall 0.5 clause) is probably right but is not the reason stated. The re-labelling of R3-a as "not discriminating" (A22c) is also post-hoc relative to A22(c), which listed it as a confirmatory test it passes. State the true reason and show queries-per-catch. The project is titled "calibration and probes", but no probe, steering, 14B, Nemo, Gemma, temperature, GPT or verbalised-confidence baseline ran, and users asked for journal-ready work: the cancellation was permitted only "on user request" for Stage 4 and is a scope gap, not a finding.
7. Weak or missing benchmarks on the same items. No verbalised-confidence baseline, no temperature sweep, no negation-targeted logprob score (see 1), no G1/chip/plain word on a non-oracle trigger, no SpeakFaster-style ranker (acknowledged). Contextual calibration is degenerate for llama by its own content-free input (6/16 negated). Two models on identical items give consistency only (stated).

## Minor issues
- Platt/contextual rows "recover R3 = YES" at 998/1,000 asks are artefacts (stated). Drop them from the table or grey them.
- 8% of greedy repeats change text, so "greedy share" and always-commit reversals (104 per 1,000) carry run-to-run noise not in the CIs; the bootstrap is over messages only.
- ECE comparison against a constant base-rate predictor (Brier 0.104 vs 0.096) lacks a CI.
- Politeness and speech-act results are unvalidated and should stay out of the abstract.
- Planned positives "about 68/16" versus actual 107/68 changes confirmatory eligibility of E-human only on a count rule; E-human remains labelled exploratory by declared rule, which is correct.
- The annotation request only checks the detector. Nothing human checks the premise that asking would resolve the cue or the AAC weights (SLP n = 2).

## Strongest claim that survives
On POS-cap4 bare keywords (negation removed in every unit), qwen2.5-7b and llama3.1-8b greedy decoding restores the negation in under 1% of the roughly 10% of messages whose source was negated (about 104 silent reversals per 1,000 messages, CI [81, 129], detector-relative), their K = 16 sampled negation share is about 0.5% to 1.6% (so default decoding is not hiding a calibrated tail), and the K = 16 sampled share ranks the at-risk inputs only slightly above chance (AUROC 0.53 to 0.59) and no better than a model-free keyword classifier.

## Next steps

2. Reframe R1 as a stress test against the cap4 construction (qwen-only headline); drop "overconfident" claims for llama until E-human resolves.
3. Cite 2511.04869 and Hou et al. 2024 after reading them; position novelty against them. Implement clustering-based semantic entropy over all variants if kept as a baseline name.
4. Remove "sensitivity 1.00" language; report FN bounds; fix 3 vs 7 arbiter disputes; add a codebook sensitivity (uncertainty and clause-level negation counted as negated).
5. Replace the oracle chip/word headline with a non-oracle trigger (for example a user who cues with probability p on truly negated messages) or label it purely as an upper bound in the abstract.
6. Run the declared 534-item human detector check and, if probes remain in scope, qwen2.5-14b and one more family before any journal claim.
