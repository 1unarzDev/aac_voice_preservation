# Frontier: "Knows but does not say" (id knows_not_says) - PLAN

Written 2026-11-02 BEFORE any generation, logprob read or look at outcomes for this frontier. Nothing in this folder
has been computed yet. All thresholds below are fixed now; any later change goes in a dated "Amendments" section at the end,
never by editing this text. Scripts will be `src/frontier_knows_not_says_*.py`; runs under `runs/frontier_knows_not_says_*`.

## 0. Question, in one paragraph
Models answer a bare keyword input with the affirmative / plain / statement reading >99% of the time (greedy). For polarity
that default is the Bayes-preferred guess (P(negated | no negation word) = 2.7% [1.8, 3.7], human keywords, D-066) but is
more confident than the evidence and costs ~24 silent reversals per 1,000 messages. Two very different explanations:
(A) the model's *distribution* is roughly calibrated to the human base rate and greedy/low-T decoding collapses it
("calibrated but hidden", a decoding problem); (B) the distribution itself is too confident (miscalibrated, a model problem);
(C) the distribution is fine on average but carries no per-input signal about WHICH inputs lost their cue (so no probe or
confidence can drive a guard). This plan separates A/B/C with logprobs and sampling, tests cheap recovery (sampled / prior-shifted
decoding, threshold-to-ask) against strong baselines, and, gated on stage results, probes hidden states.

## 1. Literature check (2026-11-02; WebSearch, snippets/abstract pages only; "not found" = a few targeted queries, not proof)
Verified at snippet level (search-result pages; not full-text read):
- "Knowing but Not Showing: LLMs Recognize Ambiguity but Rarely Ask Clarifying Questions", arXiv 2605.25284 (May 2026): models judge ambiguity with ~60-80% accuracy when asked, yet answer directly with an unstated assumption >95% of the time. Behavioral/verbalized awareness; NOT logprob or internal, NOT dimension-specific, NOT compared with a human base rate. This is our nearest framing precedent: we must cite it and not claim the phrase.
- "Why Don't You Know? Impact of Uncertainty Sources on UQ in LLMs", arXiv 2604.10495: on ambiguous inputs models commit to one interpretation with apparent confidence; near-zero clarification.
- Sparse Neurons Carry Strong Signals of Question Ambiguity in LLMs, EMNLP 2025 main (aclanthology 2025.emnlp-main.813): linear probe + ambiguity-encoding neurons; editing shifts answering to abstaining. Question-level, binary.
- Where do LLMs Encode the Knowledge to Assess the Ambiguity of Prompts?, COLING 2025 Industry (2025.coling-industry.38): MLP classifiers on last-token states, mid layers best.
- Localizing Prompt Ambiguity with Probe-Targeted Attribution (PRIG), arXiv 2606.05486: linear probe clear vs ambiguous prompts + integrated gradients.
- Zhao et al., Calibrate Before Use, ICML 2021 / arXiv 2102.09690: content-free-input prior correction (W = diag(p_cf)^-1). The closest implementable method for our prior-shift arm; its target is label bias in few-shot classification, not the omitted-cue prior of generation.
- Trained on Tokens, Calibrated on Concepts, arXiv 2511.04869: base LMs are next-token calibrated; concept-level calibration emerges. Hypothesis (not verified for chat/instruct Q4): post-training breaks this.
- Enough Coin Flips Can Make LLMs Act Bayesian, ACL 2025 (arXiv 2503.04722): token priors persist despite stated bias. The Base-Rate Effect on LLM Benchmark Performance, arXiv 2406.11634: intrinsic answer-token base rates.
- Farquhar et al., semantic entropy, Nature 630 (2024) (via ideator file, snippet-level): sample, cluster by meaning, entropy. Our sampled-disagreement baseline is a commitment-vector version of it.
- Ideator file `ideas/frontier/uncertainty_decoding.md` items 14-20, 28-33 hold the wider map (conformal, abstention, cost-aware).
Gap that remains after search (novelty, medium): (1) per-dimension (7 commitment dimensions) token/sample probability of the OMITTED reading on bare AAC keyword input, measured against an independently measured human base rate of that omission (T2 posterior), including the sign: speech act shows the model MORE extreme than the posterior; (2) a test of whether sampling/prior-shift recovers the calibrated commitment; (3) probes tied to a measured downstream silent reversal on real-source items, with cost-weighted AAC metrics. Risk: probing-for-ambiguity is crowded (4 papers above); the AAC-grounded cost/outcome link and the A/B/C separation are the contribution. Do NOT claim "first to show LLMs know but do not say" (2605.25284 exists).
Before writing the paper, re-run: "calibration of LLM sampling distribution against human base rate of omitted cue", "LLM negation omission keyword expansion AAC", "calibrated clarification policy expected cost abstention LLM generation".

## 3. Hypotheses (directional, falsifiable)
- **H1 (distribution vs decoding).** For polarity on E-human inputs with no negation word, the T=1 sampled share of negated outputs per model, p1, is within a factor of 2 of the human posterior (1.35%-5.4%), while greedy share is < 0.5%. (Alternative H1b: p1 < 1.35%, i.e. miscalibrated even undecoded.)
- **H2 (dimension contrast).** Speech act and politeness: sampled non-default share at T=1 is closer to the posterior than greedy is (T2 greedy: speech act 28% vs 48%; politeness 6.7% vs 6.5%). Prediction: speech act remains too statement-heavy (model less extreme only after temperature, but not calibrated); politeness already calibrated.
- **H3 (per-input knowledge).** Among no-negation-word bare inputs, the model's own P(negated) (sampled share K=16, and pair-score graded probability) ranks inputs whose source was negated above those whose source was not: AUROC > 0.60 (lower CI > 0.55) on E-cap4 and a lexical-only baseline (bag of keywords) is beaten by >= 0.03 AUROC. (If not: scenario C, "no per-input knowledge".)
- **H4 (recovery).** A cross-fitted prior-shifted/thresholded commitment policy reduces cost-weighted silent reversals relative to always-commit at lower taps than random-ask matched to the same tap rate (queries-per-catch <= 20 at 50% recall on E-cap4 polarity vs random-ask's ~9 on cap4 and ~37 on human, i.e. the policy must beat the matched-tap random baseline, not merely beat always-commit).
- **H5 (probe).** A linear probe on mid-layer last-input-token states predicts "source negated | no negation word" with held-out-message AUROC >= 0.75 and beats the sampled-logprob baseline (H3) by >= 0.03 AUROC; and probes of "which dimension is unspecified" on controlled channels exceed a lexical baseline after matched controls.
We expect (not a rule) A for polarity-human, partial C, and probes adding little beyond sampling. Either of A/B/C is reportable.

## 4. Experiments (staged; each <= ~1 day compute)

### Stage 0 - pilot (<= 2 h, 1 model = qwen2.5-7b, 60 E-human inputs, 20 E-pairs). Purpose: mechanics only; outcomes of H1-H5 are NOT evaluated and pilot items are excluded from stage 1 analysis.
Checks: (a) llama-server returns per-token logprobs for sampled output (`n_probs`, `/completion`, also `post_sampling_probs=false` so probs are pre-temperature/top-k); with `temperature=1, top_k=0, top_p=1, min_p=0` the sampler is truly untruncated (record the server defaults; llama.cpp default top_k=40/top_p=0.95/min_p=0.05 would silently truncate tails and bias the question, so set explicitly). (b) Whether prompt (teacher-forced) logprobs are available for candidate scoring; if not, fall back to token-by-token forced decoding with prefix cache (~15 requests per candidate; estimated 60-90 min per model per 3,000 candidates). (c) `/embedding` pooling: `--embeddings --pooling last` returns the FINAL-layer state only; mid-layer states need HF transformers (not installed; no torch in `.venv`). Install into a separate env `runs/frontier_knows_not_says_hf/` (torch + transformers + bitsandbytes 4-bit) only if Stage 4 is triggered. (d) Throughput measurement to confirm K and N below fit; scale K down (not N) if > 8 h per model.
Pass: logprobs and untruncated sampling verified on a hand-checked toy (e.g. 20 draws of a known biased prompt). Write `pilot_notes.md`.

### Stage 1 - calibration of the sampled distribution (~6-10 h total over 5 models; 1 GPU-day budget incl. waiting for lock)
- Inputs: E-human (all 1,800 sets over 600 messages; analysed on the subset with no negation word for polarity, etc., as T2) and E-cap4 (600). Prompt P0n primary, P0 secondary (E-human only).
- Per input per model: K=16 samples at T=1 (untruncated), plus one greedy (T=0) re-run for the same model/prompt to tie to the T2 numbers (expected to reproduce the stored P0 greedy within batching noise; report agreement). Also temperatures T in {0.3, 0.7} on a 200-message random subset for the temperature curve.
- Metric 1 (primary): per model and dimension, the sampled share of the cue-bearing/non-default output among inputs with no cue in the keywords, HT-weighted, 95% CI by message bootstrap (strata as T2, 10,000 resamples, seed 20261102), compared with the T2 posterior. Report ratio r = model/posterior.
- Metric 2: reliability curve (per-input sampled P(non-default), binned in quantile bins, vs observed share of source-negated), ECE and Brier with message-cluster bootstrap; pooled and per model. Expect low power on E-human polarity (~2-3% positives): E-cap4 is the main reliability dataset; E-human is shown with its CI honestly.
- Metric 3: AUROC (H3) with lexical-only baseline (L2 logistic regression on keyword unigrams, 5-fold message-grouped CV) and an output-agnostic baseline (keyword count; presence of verb/aux).
- Extra arm (decoding-problem test): pair-score graded probability on E-pairs (hw1 polarity 104 + W-C 80, bare input; candidates = variant A and B source sentences; P(B | bare) normalised over {A,B}). Reported with the caveat that it is not base-rate-comparable (design is 50/50); used only to ask whether the model's relative likelihood of the non-default reading is near 0.5 (indifferent), 0.97+ (confident default), or in between, against the sampled share on the same inputs.
- GPT (codex-lb, concurrency <= 4) and Gemini: sampled-frequency analogue on a 600-input subset, K=8, T=1, labelled "sampling only, not logprobs"; skipped if codex-lb is down.
- Strong baselines for the *probability* itself: (i) human base rate (the target), (ii) uniform / always-default, (iii) Calibrate-before-use content-free estimate (`N/A`, empty keywords) as a prior estimator, (iv) the model's verbalized "is the polarity unspecified? yes/no + P(negated)" prompt (same input; verbalized confidence baseline).

### Stage 2 - recovery decoders and policies (offline on Stage 1 stored samples; +1-3 h GPU for a few new arms; same items)
Policies, each producing commit-default / commit-alternative / ask per input (all simulated against the source label; ask = resolves correctly):
1. always-commit (greedy default) - the study's status quo; 2. always-ask; 3. random-ask matched to each policy's tap rate (200 draws); 4. current-study interventions on the same items where applicable: plain-word cue and chip (cue supplied, 0 taps for the model but 1 for the user = counted as an ask) and guard G1 (judge-driven monitor + regenerate; only on E-pairs/hw1/W-C where it was run, reported separately, NOT forced onto E-human);
5. **sampled-commit**: pick the label by majority of K=16 at T=1; 6. **sample-disagreement ask** (semantic-entropy analogue): ask when share of non-default among K >= t; 7. **prior-shift**: logit-shift of the non-default class by log(pi_human/p_model_bare) fitted on a calibration half (and the Calibrate-before-use content-free variant) then threshold; 8. **cost-optimal threshold**: ask when P'(non-default) * w_reversal > (1 - P') * w_spurious + tap_cost, with weights from the SLP table. Thresholds/shifts are fitted on a random half of MESSAGES (stratified by sample type) and evaluated on the other half, swapped (2-fold, repeated 20x). Closest published method implemented: contextual calibration (Zhao 2021) as arm 7; semantic-entropy-style disagreement as arm 6; verbalized confidence as baseline. State this in the paper.
- Metrics, all on the same items with message-cluster bootstrap CIs and paired differences vs always-commit: silent reversals per 1,000 messages (unweighted and SLP-weighted), spurious cue insertions per 1,000, taps per 1,000, queries per catch, recall at fixed tap budgets (5, 10, 25 per 100), and a Pareto curve (reversals vs taps) with random-ask and always-ask endpoints. Acceptability: no new human data; only a tap-count proxy (state it).
- Report the "does not help" cases explicitly: speech act (posterior ~48%, no safe default; ask rate is high), dimensions where the model is calibrated at greedy, and any model where per-input AUROC <= 0.55.

### Stage 3 - scale-up and robustness (<= 1 day, only if Stage 1/2 show something to scale)
Run the criteria of Stage 1 on +2,400 AACText messages with POS-cap4 for qwen2.5-7b and llama3.1-8b (K=16, ~77k generations per model) to tighten reliability curves; legend arm (P0) replication; 14B/Nemo confirmation of any recovery result. If the stage 1 result is "no signal" for every model, Stage 3 is cancelled (nothing to scale).

### Stage 4 - hidden-state probes (GATED; <= 1 day; only if Stage 1 gives scenario A/C with H3 AUROC >= 0.60 for at least 2 models, or if the user asks)
- Env: HF transformers 4-bit for qwen2.5-7b and llama3.1-8b (14B only to confirm), activations at the last input token (end of the prompt before generation), every 4th layer, saved fp16; no generation needed (one forward per input; E-cap4 + E-human + E-pairs ~ 5,000 inputs; ~1 h per model). Logistic probes (L2; sklearn installed in the HF env), layer chosen on a validation split of MESSAGES, never on test.
- Probe tasks: (P-a) **which dimension is unspecified** on controlled channels (bare vs chip-supplied vs plain-word-supplied, per dimension, from hw1/W-C), evaluated on held-out WRITERS (hw1) and with matched controls: lexical-only baseline, cue-token-masked inputs, shuffled labels, random-direction and layer-0 controls (so a probe that reads only "a negation word is present" is exposed); (P-b) **will a silent reversal follow**: target = source negated | no negation word (E-cap4/E-human), held-out messages; compared against sampled share (H3 baseline), verbalized judgement, and lexical L2 baseline; (P-c) optional steering along the (P-a) direction at layer L, alpha in {0, 1, 2, 4}; measure share of negated / hedged outputs vs fluency (detector + 100 outputs labelled by judge+arbiter); reported as exploratory.
- Also llama-server `/embedding` (final layer) as the "cheap deployable" probe on the same labels, to show whether the final-layer state suffices.
- Probe-gated guard (exploratory): ask only when probe score >= threshold, compared on the Stage 2 Pareto.

## 5. PRE-DECLARED decision rules and success criteria
Unit of analysis is the message; CIs by message bootstrap (10,000, seed 20261102), Holm correction across the 5 models for each primary claim; a claim needs the rule to hold in the pooled estimate AND the stated number of models.
**R1 (A/B/C classification, polarity, E-human, P0n, T=1).** For each model m: ratio r_m = sampled negated share / 2.7%.
- m "calibrated-but-hidden": r_m in [0.5, 2] with the 95% CI of the share not entirely below 1.35%, AND greedy share < 0.5%.
- m "miscalibrated (overconfident)": the 95% CI upper bound of the sampled share < 1.35%.
- m "under-confident": CI lower bound > 5.4%.
- Headline "decoding problem" if >= 3 of 5 models are calibrated-but-hidden; headline "miscalibrated" if >= 3 of 5 are overconfident; otherwise "mixed, per model". Same rule on E-cap4 with target 11.4% (interval [5.7%, 22.8%]) as a replication; if E-human and E-cap4 disagree, report the disagreement and do not headline either way.
**R2 (per-input knowledge).** "Knows which inputs" if pooled AUROC (E-cap4, polarity) lower CI > 0.55 AND exceeds the lexical baseline by >= 0.03 with paired CI excluding 0 in >= 3 models. Else scenario C: calibrated-on-average without discriminating, and Stage 4 is run only on user request.
**R3 (recovery).** A decoder/policy "recovers" if, on held-out folds and E-cap4 polarity, its cost-weighted reversals per 1,000 are lower than always-commit with paired CI excluding 0 AND at the same tap rate its caught reversals exceed random-ask by a paired CI excluding 0 AND queries-per-catch <= 20 at recall 0.5. Sampled-commit alone "recovers" only if it lowers reversals at no more spurious insertions than +1 per 1,000. If no policy passes, say so and report the best one as non-recovering. Success statement "improvement in AAC terms" is allowed only for passing policies, in reversals / weighted reversals / taps per 1,000, always labelled "simulated, assumes asking resolves the cue".
**R4 (probe).** H5 holds if held-out-message AUROC >= 0.75 AND > sampled-logprob baseline by >= 0.03 (paired CI excludes 0) in >= 2 families, AND the P-a probes beat the cue-masked/lexical controls by >= 0.05 AUROC. Else the paper reports "probes add nothing beyond behaviour/lexical cues" (a publishable null). Steering is exploratory and never headlined without human-checked fluency.
**R5 (honest scope).** No claim about people or real-world benefit; dimension claims only where a real-text base rate exists (polarity, politeness, speech act); other dimensions are descriptive. Chat-template Q4 instruct models only. No J1 fallback; detector labels flagged where used; Part 21 reweighting reported when available.
**Stop rules.** Stage 0 fails -> fix mechanics, no outcome look. Stage 1 abandons a model if > 5% request errors. If R1 and R2 are both negative for all models and R3 fails, finish with the clean negative ("miscalibrated and uninformative; only the cue/ask route works") and cancel Stages 3-4.

### Amendments (dated 2026-11-02, made before any pilot output; label "pre-outcome")
A1. Stage 0 numeric pass rule: (a) logprobs returned for >= 99% of generated tokens; (b) sampling with top_k=0, top_p=1, min_p=0 gives per-token empirical frequency within 0.03 of the server probability on a 20-prompt x 200-sample check; (c) teacher-forced scorer reproduces the sampled-token logprob within 0.05 nats on >= 95% of 50 checked sequences (not 1); (d) /embedding returns finite vectors of fixed dimension; (e) throughput measured and Stage 1 time re-estimated. Remove the dead has_prompt_logprobs check. Any fail -> fix or drop that arm; record it.
A2. All AUROC, ECE and probe metrics are Horvitz-Thompson weighted by the inverse inclusion probability of each enriched stratum, with bootstrap CIs resampling items (not generations). Unweighted versions reported only as secondary.
A3. R3 (queries-per-catch) gets a per-dataset bar: it must beat random-ask AT THE SAME ask rate, with paired item-bootstrap CI excluding 0; the fixed "<= 20" bar is dropped.
A4. R1 is recast as an equivalence test: calibrated if the 90% CI of the sampled-share/target ratio lies within [0.67, 1.5]; miscalibrated if it lies wholly outside [0.5, 2]; otherwise INCONCLUSIVE (reported as such). Headline requires R1 and R2 together.
A5. Replication unit: models share items, so "k of 5 models" is reported as consistency, not independent replication. The inferential unit is the item; model is a fixed factor with heterogeneity shown. Power is stated per dataset (about 68 cap4 positives, about 16 human) and underpowered cells are labelled exploratory.
A6. Baselines added on the same items: (i) greedy first-token entropy and mean token entropy (single pass), (ii) a stronger lexical baseline (length/keyword ratio plus source-cue features, logistic, cross-validated), (iii) always-commit, random-ask, always-ask, G1, cue chip, plain word, with SLP-weighted silent reversals per 1,000 messages and taps per 1,000.
A7. Pair-scoring arm: score divergent-span and length-normalised logprobs, add a placebo contrast (paraphrase pair with no meaning change). 0.5 is not read as "indifferent" without the placebo calibration.
A8. Detector-label correlation: source and output share a detector. R1/R2/headline numbers stay PROVISIONAL and are not headlined until Part 21 (source-label check) is incorporated. If Part 21 is not back by Stage 2, report both detector-based and Part-21-corrected numbers.
A9. Literature: run the remaining queries (SpeakFaster / AAC abbreviation expansion, RSA / omitted-content priors) and reconsider the title because of closeness to arXiv 2605.25284 (propose "Which reading does the model drop? Sampling-distribution calibration for AAC message expansion" or similar). Cite only verified sources.
