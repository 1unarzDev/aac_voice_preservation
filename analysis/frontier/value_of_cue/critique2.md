# value_of_cue, round 2: adversarial critique (methods/statistics + novelty)

Verdict: valid = TRUE. I found no fatal issue. The round-2 numbers are internally consistent and the write-up is honest about H2 not being met, ASK never being chosen, and P-O absorbing most of the internal-signal gain. There are several MAJOR issues that block any journal claim of "internal signals help" or "beats published methods".

## Major issues
1. Leakage fix incomplete (above). H2 is stated as a delta over P-K, and P-I/P-O are built on the same leaky S1, so deltas are probably less affected, but every reported absolute AUROC for speech act and politeness, and the claim "keyword-only P-K AUROC .73 / .71", should be restated with the strict set. Source `nocue_*` flags are lexicon-defined for polarity and politeness; the outcome is partly a function of keyword text.
2. Headline rests on an unmeasured q = 0.9 and on a one-sided cost model. SHOW-TWO gain is credited at q on true errors only. False-alarm prompts (about 90% of the roughly 10% prompted messages are not errors for most dimensions) carry no cost, yet a user who mis-taps the alternative would introduce a reversal. Spurious additions are excluded from the primary outcome altogether. ASK never being chosen is mechanical (2 taps vs 1 tap at q = .9 vs rho .79 to .995), so "value-of-cue" is a show-two-alternatives result. The alternative sentence is never shown to cover the true speech act (multi-class, a single flip). The annotation request that would measure q is unsent, so all AAC-unit numbers (26 weighted / 32.6 raw errors averted per 1,000, 3.1 taps per error) are conditional simulations, not outcomes.
3. The H4 comparison to the "closest published method" is weak and partly unfair. B6 is an unsupervised entropy gate (K = 8 samples, labelled by L0, not the arbiter), compared against supervised logistic policies trained on outcome labels. It is not a reproduction (source never opened), and its raw signal AUROC is .51 to .59. "P-I beats B6 by +10 pp" mostly shows supervision beats zero-shot. A fair baseline is a supervised model on S1 + the B6 entropy/minority features alone, which would be a small refit. No G1 comparison exists beyond a transfer assumption.
4. The honest control undercuts the internal-signal claim, and the control is itself imperfect. P-I minus P-O is +2.5 [-2.8, 7.8], so no gain from logprobs/sampling/probes beyond the committed output is shown. P-O uses L0 labels of a regenerated greedy text that differs from the stored output in about 8.5 to 9% of units (identical-text rate 91 to 92%), whereas the outcome uses arbitrated labels on the stored text. P-O is therefore handicapped by label noise and mismatch (a P-O built from the stored-output arbiter label would be a stronger control), and P-I includes the same noisy features. Net: the "P-I 14.6%, P-ALL 15.4%" headline should be reported as "ask when the model's own output lacks the cue", not as an internal-uncertainty result. Sign of the bias favours the paper only if P-O is weak; stating the stronger P-O would likely close the 2.5 pp gap.
5. Stop rule and selection. Best-of-four (P-K/P-X/P-I/P-ALL) is chosen on the same data it is tested on (winner's curse, small); the stop rule passed with CI [3.9, 13.4] on pooled outcomes from 2 of 3 declared local models (gemma not run) and 20 writers. The bootstrap holds the fitted policy fixed (ignores model-fit variance), the B5 prior uses all 1,800 units (favours B5, conservative), and B5 CI [.059, .072] and B1 (analytic) have no real sampling interval, so differences against them are paired but their own bars are not comparable. Pooled outcome averages 4 models (including gpt-6.1-terra) while features come from only 2 local models.
6. Post-pilot control A3 and several scope changes were made after seeing outcomes (primary scope re-chosen in round 1, A3 after a 30-resample qwen run that already showed P-I AUROC .82 to .85). A3 is declared as post-pilot, the stop-rule set was not changed, and H2 is reported as not met, so this is disclosed, not hidden. But the headline "primary scope" is a post-hoc choice, and the all-five-dimension result (P-K 3.9% vs B5 4.0%, no gain) is arguably the pre-planned one. Lead with both.
7. Effect size in AAC terms is modest and uneven. Per writer, P-K beats B5 for only 7 of 20 writers (median gain 0.6%), prompts concentrate on writers who drop cues. Polarity (weight 1.0) AUROC CI includes .5 for every signal set: no per-message signal where severity is highest. H3 operating points need 0.5 to 1.7 prompts per message with 31 to 40% exceedance and are not exchangeable, so "calibrated guarantee" must not appear in a paper.

## Minor issues
- Source cue flags are detector labels (Part 21 human check unscored); agent/certainty include detector-vs-judge disagreement.
- K = 8 (not 16); gemma3-4b missing; H2 "2 of 3 models" rule cannot be evaluated as declared.
- Cache and seeds are logged only via SEED constants; no per-run manifest.
- Oracle is one-action-only and unrealisable; do not present 42.7% as headroom.
- B6 source unverified (OpenReview blocked); all [S] citations must be opened before use.

## Novelty (WebSearch 2026-11-02)
Search for AAC keyword expansion plus clarification/ask-vs-commit again found no AAC paper with a cost-priced ask-vs-commit rule. Related work: Cai et al. NAACL 2022 (abbreviation expansion with several candidate options), SpeakFaster (Nature Communications 2024), KwickChat (IUI 2022), Valencia et al. CHI 2023, and the project's own repo. Chow-rule abstention, entropy-gated clarification and conformal risk control are generic and known. Novelty remains medium-to-low: setting, SLP weights and tap pricing; the same-items comparison of signal types is not supported by H2 (internal beats external fails on 1 of 2 models).

## Strongest true claim
On 1,800 human-keyword units (20 writers, 600 messages; machine-arbitrated, provisional labels), a supervised policy that shows a cue-flipped alternative on about 10% of messages (100 taps per 1,000, assumed q = 0.9) averts 12 to 15% of SLP-weighted silent errors (P-O 12.1, P-I 14.6, P-ALL 15.4) against 6.5% for the prior-only rule, with paired CI for P-I minus B5 [3.4, 12.9]; most of that gain comes from knowing the committed output lacks a cue, not from logprob or sampling uncertainty, and it does not help polarity.
