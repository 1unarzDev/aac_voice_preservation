Verdict: valid = TRUE (no fatal issue; the Stage-0 numbers reproduce and the write-up is mostly honest about being a modest, provisional pilot). There are several MAJOR issues that must be fixed before any journal claim.

## Major issues
1. ASK-ONE-CUE (the chip, the study's own intervention) is almost never chosen. With q=0.9 at 1 tap vs rho .79-.995 at 2 taps, SHOW-TWO dominates ASK for speech act and politeness at every lambda. The "rho=1" sensitivity is identical to the primary result (5.6%), confirming this. So the "value-of-cue" frontier is really a show-two-alternatives frontier. The headline depends on q=0.9, an invented constant never measured (q=0.7 gives 4.3%). Also the alternative sentence for speech act or agent is never shown to cover the true meaning. Must reframe, or measure q (SLP or non-author annotators on real alternatives).
2. H3 "SUCCESS as pre-declared" is overclaimed. (a) The success rule was amended after the feasibility computation. (b) The "always-ask floor 47" justification is wrong: the lambda=0 policy attains 25.5 (show-two for speech act and politeness), so the real reason alpha=5/10/20 fail is a floor near 25, not 47. (c) B5 in CRC is a deterministic ladder with a different definition from frontier B5 (random fraction); it overshoots (risk 81/115/150 vs alpha 100/150/250). The "38% fewer taps than B5" is largely this granularity, and the report admits it only for alpha=250. Compare at equal realised risk or give B5 randomisation. (d) P-K vs B1 test risk is equal at alpha=100 (92.7 vs 91.9) so the taps saving is the only evidence; 1-3 prompts per message (1,000-2,800 taps/1,000) is not an AAC-usable operating point, so "guarantee holds" carries no practical value. (e) Plain CRC finite-sample correction is applied to HT-weighted, non-exchangeable units (3 keyword sets per message, 20 writers) and the P-K predictions are cross-fitted on blocks that include the calibration and test units; exchangeability is assumed, not argued. 35-39% split violation is reported (good).
3. About 41% of the B0 burden (agent 158.8 + certainty 26.5 of 451.9) comes from the two dimensions the report itself calls least trustworthy (detector-vs-judge label disagreement, not a reversal of meaning). The headline 5.6% and all tap/averted numbers include them; the 10.1% exclusion variant uses a different denominator. Politeness errors are mostly spurious additions (75.2 spurious vs 49.4 reversals) and ASK/SHOW-TWO are assumed to fix them at rho/q measured on LOST cues only. Report primary results for polarity + speech act + politeness-lost only, with agent/certainty as exploratory.
4. Benchmarking is incomplete versus the stated goal: no closest-published-method baseline (B6 entropy-gating not run), no G1 comparison, no P-I/P-X signals, no hw1/hw2 transfer, no human validation. H2 and H4 are OPEN, so no claim against prior methods or of "internal vs external" is allowed. The figure shows only P-K, B5, B1 and an oracle; no CI bands, curves cut at 600 taps, B1 "random" is a weak strawman (analytic, better of ASK/SHOW-TWO). Add at least one cheap strong heuristic (e.g. keyword-count or "no function word" rule, per-dimension prior ladder with randomisation).
5. Effect is small in AAC terms and the framing should say so: 25.3 weighted errors averted per 1,000 messages at 100 taps (5.6% of B0); P-K minus prior-only B5 is +2.0 pp [0.4, 3.8] and only 75% of random SLP weight vectors favour P-K over B5. B5 prior-only gets 64% of P-K's gain. Excluding certainty/agent, B5 is 6.1% vs P-K 10.1%. Polarity (the most severe dimension) AUROC 0.60 [0.43, 0.75] and certainty 0.59 [0.45, 0.71] cross 0.5: no per-message signal where severity is highest. Politeness at T=20 is below random.

## Minor issues
- Feature leakage by construction: r_polarity requires "no NEG lexicon hit in keywords", and S1 includes exactly that NEG/POL/ACT regex as a feature; the model partly learns the outcome definition (excludes r=1) rather than risk. Not leakage of the outcome from outputs, but inflates AUROC for polarity/politeness/speech act relative to a policy that genuinely sees only keywords; state it.
- B5 prior and HT base rates use all 1,800 units (favours B5; conservative for the P-K advantage). Inner-CV for C is grouped by message only. Bootstrap holds the fitted policy fixed (ignores model-fit variance); "widest of three" CIs are conservative, but 20 writers remain thin. Per-dimension results have no CIs.
- Y averages 4 models (fractional outcome), so the "stable across models" claim uses same-P policies; the 4 models are near-identical on polarity.
- B1 has no CI by construction yet "P-K minus B1" CI is reported; fine but say B1 is analytic.
- Oracle uses realised Y with one action per message; it bounds one-action policies only. The 5.6% -> 16.8% gap is mostly agent/speech act where the oracle is unrealisable.
- Round1.md says log is empty; no run log of seeds beyond SEED. Fragile id()-keyed cache should be replaced.
- Annotation request not built; fine for Stage 0 but items should include policy-miss strata plus an alternative-sentence adequacy check (issue 1).

## Novelty (WebSearch 2026-11-02)
Searches again found no AAC paper with a cost-priced, SLP-weighted ask-vs-commit rule; closest remain Cai et al. 2022 (arXiv 2205.03767; suggests multiple options and confirmation, notes correction cost) and SpeakFaster (Nature Comms 2024, FillMask correction), plus a 2026 AAC Journal "Ability adaptive AAC" paper whose clarification layer uses a confidence threshold (verify by opening; it is a design paper, not an evaluation). The PLAN's [S] citations (Clarify When Necessary authors, arXiv 2606.29054, 2607.27143, 2609.07395) were seen only in search snippets and must be opened before citation. Generic ask-vs-commit (Chow rule, entropy-gated clarification, conformal risk control) is well-known; novelty is only the setting, SLP weights and measured costs, and that rests on issues 1-4. Novelty: medium to low until B6 and measured q exist.

## Strongest true claim
On 1,800 human-keyword units (20 writers, 600 messages), a keyword-only logistic policy chosen from held-out blocks averts 5.6% [4.0, 7.5] of SLP-weighted silent errors at 100 taps/1,000 messages (about 25 weighted per 1,000), significantly more than random asking (1.8%) and prior-only asking (3.6%), robust to SLP weights; most of that gain is show-two on speech act and agent under an assumed q=0.9, and it is far below the 25% pre-declared success line.
