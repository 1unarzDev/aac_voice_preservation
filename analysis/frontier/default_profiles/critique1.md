# default_profiles: adversarial critique, round 1 (methods/statistics + novelty)

Verdict: valid = false. One fatal conceptual issue (the polarity "calibration gap" compares a greedy output rate to a posterior), several major issues. Machine-only and provisional status is correctly stated; the problems are in what is claimed from it.

Checks re-run by the critic: eligible-share and gated-random computation using the unit construction from `src/frontier_default_profiles_policy.py` (no outputs overwritten); per-model prior-vs-confidence contrasts read from `policy_paired_contrasts.csv`; `logprob_calibration.csv`.

## FATAL
1. **H1 "gap" is not miscalibration for polarity.** Gap = posterior P(cue | no cue in keywords) minus the model's greedy (argmax) cue-assertion rate. A perfectly calibrated model that decodes greedily outputs "no negation" on essentially every one of those units, because the posterior is 2.7%. A zero greedy rate (the models: about 0.07-0.33%) is the Bayes-optimal 0-1 decision, so the +2.5 pp gap (I^2 = 0%) is simply the base rate minus about 0, and is guaranteed for any competent model. "The profile is a property of the task, not a model" is true but trivial, and "models are more confident than the posterior supports" is not shown. The report's own logprob probe points the other way: mean q(negated | no negation word) is 0.37-0.49 (`logprob_calibration.csv`) versus a 2.7% posterior, i.e. models are heavily under-confident on the belief scale. The report notes the scales differ but still states the overconfidence headline. A calibration claim needs sampled-output rates (T>0, many draws) or a proper belief readout; speech act (posterior about 48%, where argmax is a genuine choice) is the only dimension where a greedy gap is informative.

## MAJOR
2. **H1 sign convention changed after outcomes.** PLAN section 1 defines G = P(model asserts cue) - pi, and section 4 H1 wrote polarity positive and speech act negative (<= -10 pp). Round 1 redefines G as pi minus model rate so that both are positive and declares "SUPPORTED as pre-specified in spirit". Under the plan's literal section 1 definition polarity is negative (-2.5), contradicting the literal H1 polarity clause. This is a deviation and should read "H1 as written: not met for polarity; met for speech act with the sign as written; the redefined version is post hoc".
3. **The "closest published implementable comparator" has failed its own validity stop rule and no valid substitute exists.** Stop rule (Spearman q vs judged output < 0.2) triggered in 8 of 9 cells; the plan said drop the logprob arm and keep the verbalized arm; the verbalized arm was not run. H4's headline comparison ("prior matches model confidence") is therefore against a comparator declared invalid, and the benchmark requirement (strong prior-method baseline) is unmet. Also, the stop rule is ill-suited at floor (polarity positive outputs about 0.1%, so Spearman about 0 is near-mechanical); say so rather than treat it as informative either way. The probe also had pilot-phase changes (A/B letters discarded) and q0 added after viewing results (disclosed forking path).
4. **Misreported contrast: politeness prior vs confidence.** The report says "vs confidence not significant". `policy_paired_contrasts.csv` shows prior minus conf_gated at 10% of -9.1 to -10.5 per 1,000 with CIs excluding 0 for every single model and the 3-model mean (e.g. mean -9.8 [-19.6, -2.0]). It is about 20% fewer reversals (below the 25% bar but significant). Correct the text.
5. **Per-model confidence is sometimes better than the prior on polarity** (qwen2.5-7b gated 14.3 vs prior 17.7 at 10%; diff +3.4 [-2.2, 9.7], not significant), and the 3-model mean was chosen as the comparator. Averaging the models dilutes the best one; the correct reading is "prior and best single model confidence are not distinguishable", with CIs wide enough (about +-6) that a 25% difference cannot be excluded in either direction. Do not claim equivalence; the study is underpowered (76 polarity exposure units).
6. **Gate asymmetry in the baseline set.** Prior, shortest-first and confidence are lexically gated (units with the cue word in the keywords are never asked), while "random" asks over all units and wastes asks. The gate for polarity and politeness is exactly the definition of E (E = source cue AND no lexical cue), so ungated random is handicapped by construction. Critic recomputation of 10% gated random: polarity 21.7 (ungated 21.9), politeness 49.9 (50.7), speech act 240.7 (249.7); eligible shares 91%, 87%, 66%. The effect is small, so conclusions survive, but a gated-random row should be the reported baseline. Also for speech act, 52 of 417 exposure units (35.8 per 1,000) are lexically gated out under ACT regex that includes "is/do/can/want/need", so no gated policy can ever catch them; the speech-act ceiling is policy-independent artefact of the regex.
7. **Speech-act and politeness exposure rest on detector proxies**, and 277.5 vs 161.9 per 1,000 shows exposure overstates model-visible reversals by about 40%; the policy tables lead with exposure. Lead with the model-output event or state both as bounds in the top table. Part 21 source check not yet scored.
8. **No same-items comparison with G1, chip, plain word**, which is the central benchmark ask (silent reversals per 1,000, taps). The report explains why G1 cannot act on bare input, but the study interventions are then benchmarked only by reference to other items (H3 restoration effects). This leaves "improvement in AAC terms" unquantified for the interventions. Taps/1,000 not computed. A policy whose best result is 6.7 reversals avoided per 1,000 messages at 100 asks (about 15 asks per catch, human-keyword users) is a small practical effect and should not be framed as an improvement without a cost model (lambda is never specified, and "cost weighting is irrelevant within a dimension" by the report's own limit, so the "base-rate/cost policy" label overstates; it is a base-rate prior).
9. **Placebo H3 is mechanical for polarity and politeness** (disclosed): CI [0.0, 0.0] is a degenerate result from the A/B design, not evidence. "Credited yes 6/6" for those dimensions should read "not testable". Only speech act, certainty, quantity are informative, and they fail. The remaining positive causal claim is therefore only for agent. Also agent was not a primary dimension.
10. **Bootstrap understatement.** CV prior scores are not refit inside the bootstrap, single seed/fold split, hyperparameter lam = 1 untuned, and mixed-dimension multiplicity (3 dims x 4 budgets x 2 scopes) is uncorrected for the paired contrasts; H4 was declared as primary only for polarity, but the politeness and speech-act paired lines are exploratory and should be labelled so. The DerSimonian-Laird RE across 4 models with shared items is mis-specified (acknowledged); I^2 = 87% for politeness is driven by gemma and uninterpretable.
11. **Scope shortfall vs the plan**: 3 of 5 probe models; profile on 4 models (plan: 6); S3 (RG/PPI), S4 DIF, verbalized arm, Gemini not run; H5 and H6 have no result. Gold n is 24-39 units (CI half-width > .07), so trigger 5.3 fires and an ANNOTATION_REQUEST.md should now exist (the plan says to write it; the gitstatus lists the file only for other frontiers).

## Minor
- H2 null is honest, but the polarity/politeness order depends on one SLP rater; two raters and weights NEG 5/5 are not a severity scale; keep it descriptive. Also clinical price counts speech-act "reversals" with weight 0.8 although the speech-act source label is a detector proxy.
- Examples and "about 15 asks per reversal" use a model-independent exposure event and the assumption that asking always restores the cue.
- Spearman uses binary y with a floor rate; use AUROC with a bootstrap CI as primary.

## Novelty (search, 2026-11-02, unverified beyond titles)
Selective clarification triggered by an ambiguity classifier or by uncertainty is established: CLAM (Kuhn et al., arXiv 2212.07769) uses an ambiguity classifier with a tuned threshold; "Clarify When Necessary" (Zhang and Choi) and "Modeling Future Conversation Turns" (arXiv 2410.13788) compare ambiguity predictors against other ask-triggers. The proposed prior (a text-feature classifier that predicts when the cue was lost) is the same family; the genuine novelty is (a) the human base rate from keyword-to-message data and (b) pricing in silent reversals, SLP-weighted. Do not claim the policy idea. Also cite CLAM as the actual closest implementable comparator (ambiguity classifier plus threshold) rather than only logprob confidence. Already listed in PLAN: Wang and Suresh; this critic did not re-verify them.

## Strongest true claim
On 1,800 classmate keyword sets, a no-model-call lexical prior cuts polarity silent reversals from 24.4 to 17.7 per 1,000 messages at a 10% ask budget (19.5% fewer than random asks, CI [3%, 40%]; politeness 20% fewer than confidence, CI excluding 0), while on speech act no policy helps beyond about 20% and the prior is indistinguishable from shortest-first and from model confidence. Polarity speech-act "default" profiles are real but the polarity gap is the base rate itself under greedy decoding.
