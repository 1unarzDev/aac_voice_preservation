# Partner-context contamination of AAC defaults (id partner_context): final director report

Status: PROVISIONAL. All outcome labels are machine labels (two gpt judges plus a blind arbiter, no J1 fallback). The human check (ANNOTATION_REQUEST.md, round-3 section; items and key built, NOT sent) has not returned. Three critic rounds ran; the round cap was reached (D-R3, STOP). Sources: PLAN.md (A1-A10b, D-R1..D-R3), round1.md, round2.md, round3.md, critique1-3.md, r3_summary.json, r3_*.csv, r3_fig1..8. The earlier draft is kept as REPORT_round3_draft.md; this file supersedes it and applies the round-3 critic's wording fixes.

## 1. Question
When a keyword-to-sentence model is given the conversation partner's last remark (the Cai et al. 2022 "context in the prompt" idea, which improves expansion accuracy), does the remark leak into what the user is made to say, in particular as a silent polarity reversal (affirmative intent rendered negated)? Does a supplied cue (chip, plain word) stop it? Does any mitigation beat naive baselines?

## 2. Pre-declared rules, and what changed
The original plan (PLAN.md sections 1-5, written before any partner turn existed) fixed hypotheses H1-H6, contrasts C1-C4, a +-2 pp equivalence margin, rules R1-R9 and the arms M0-M5. Honest history:
- Round 1 (pilot, 40 families) failed the first feasibility gate (checker pass 80% vs 85%), fixed by template/checker rewording before any model output (A2). Critic 1 judged the pilot invalid (valid=false): the neutral turn N was a defective baseline (35% of families dropped, selection on the baseline), the bare-channel "benefit" contrast C4 is the same number as C2 by construction, the q*=0.5 break-even is trivial, turns were authored by the model under test, and Q turns rewrote the topic. Round 1 effects of 12 to 54% came from a fixed template phrase and are NOT reported as findings.
- Rounds 2-3 (D-R1, D-R2, A3-A10b) were post-pilot and outcome-informed, and are labelled so: baseline became the no-turn arm; turns authored by a different model family (mistral-nemo-12b; qwen2.5-14b on 40 families); X split into Xa explicit negation, Xb implicit, Xc quantifier; an echo/agreement-reply judge was added; topic-locked Q turns; the equivalence margin was widened from +-2 to +-5 pp after the pilot (stated wherever used; nothing is called "equivalent" or "negligible"). C4 was demoted (circular). The confirmatory pre-declared rule R6 (run E2 only if C1 or C2 >= 5 pp) was met.
- Not run: the entropy/logprob-gated ask (stored generations have no logprobs), speech-act and certainty dimensions, Gemini, a second turn author on all 130 families.

## 3. What was run
130 polarity families (60 human-written by 7 writers, 70 constructed; pilot families excluded), 4 models (qwen2.5-7b, llama3.1-8b, gemma3-4b, gpt-6.1-terra), partner turns D, Xa, Xb, Xc, Q plus a no-turn arm 0; channels bare, chip, plain word; prompts P0, P0c (turn in context), P0cb (M4 "background only" instruction). Statistics: family-cluster percentile bootstrap, B=5000, seed 20261103, pooled mean of 4 models (per-model values shown); writer-cluster bootstrap as robustness. Roughly 20 exploratory contrasts were examined; Holm covers only the three confirmatory ones. One greedy draw; terra is not exactly reproducible. Turns are model-written templates, not natural dialogue.

## 4. Results (pp = percentage points, 95% CI)
Bare channel, a negative remark (X) minus no-turn, intended-affirmative messages (C2):
- All negated output: +36.7 [32.6, 40.7] (per model 44.1 / 44.6 / 39.7 / 18.2 for qwen / llama / gemma / terra).
- Excluding verbatim echoes and agreement replies ("propositional"): +8.7 [6.9, 10.6] (8.7 / 13.1 / 9.2 / 3.8).
- Cleaner forms Xa+Xc only: +6.8 [4.9, 8.9]; checker-passing turns +6.4 [4.3, 8.6]. Xb alone is +12.5 ITT but +6.4 [2.5, 11.3] on checker-passing Xb: the Xb excess is a lexical-form artefact.
- AUTHOR DEPENDENCE (limit belongs in any abstract): on the 40 families with two authors, mistral-authored +9.2 [5.8, 13.0] vs qwen14-authored +4.2 [2.3, 6.3] (writer-cluster CI [2.8, 6.1]); the qwen14 effect is smaller in all 4 models, CI includes 0 for llama. Only two authors, second author on 40 of 130 families. Conservative lower end over all writer-cluster rows: +2.8.
- The M4 instruction cuts the propositional excess to +4.3 [3.1, 5.6] (terra -0.5).
- Of intended-affirmative outputs written negated, 44 / 45 / 40 / 19% follow an X turn and are echoes or agreement replies (qwen / llama / gemma / terra), versus under 1% with no turn (r2_decomp.csv, fig1).
- Machine decomposition of the 142 propositional negations (paraphrase of partner 71 incl. 15 unresolved, fabrication 27, partner-independent 44) is INDICATIVE ONLY: the matched affirmative control (60 outputs) gives 13% / 18% / 68%, so fabrication (19% vs 18%) is indistinguishable from judge noise, and the 15 unresolved were counted as paraphrase (56 of 142 = 39% without them). No attribution in pp by class is claimed.

Cue supplied (chip [YES]), X minus no-turn (C1):
- PRIMARY, echoes and agreement replies counted as harm: +7.05 [5.26, 9.04] (qwen +9.5, llama +2.8, gemma +12.3, terra +3.6; all per-model CIs exclude 0). Arm 0 baseline about 2.4 pp.
- Lower bound, echo/reply excluded: +1.22 [0.19, 2.24] (arm 0 unfiltered) and +1.54 [0.83, 2.37] (arm 0 filtered symmetrically; only 5 arm-0 outputs in scope, so this filter is nearly vacuous). Inside +-5 pp but not inside the original +-2 pp. Whether an agreement reply under a cue is harm is the human question. Classification under rule R1/R2: contamination detectable, not rejected.

Question frame (C3): H3 is not supported. Q minus no-turn: affirmative share -6.0 [-8.3, -3.7], of which abstention +4.4 [2.5, 6.5] (qwen +10.8); non-abstained affirmative share -2.2 [-3.7, -0.8] (llama carries it, -6.2). A Q remark lowers usable output; it does not raise the default reading.

Label robustness: C2 all-negation final +36.67, J1-only +36.54, J2-only +36.86; C1 unfiltered 7.05 / 7.12 / 7.05. Judge choice moves nothing. Drift note: shared round-3 code gives author subsets +9.2/+4.2 and pooled 36.67; round-2 quotes (+9.8/+4.8, 37.1) are superseded, cause untraced.

## 5. Benchmarks against baselines (X-frame; q = assumed share of remarks whose frame contradicts the user's intent; q is NOT measured, so every ranking below is a function of q and is not a prevalence claim)
Wrong polarity per 1,000 X-frame messages at q=0.1: always commit with context (Cai-style M0) 575; M4 instruction 777; ask-if-output-changes with the turn (M5b, 402 taps) 544 vs random ask at the same 402 taps 362 (M5b +181 [163, 199] worse). Same-tap comparisons only are credited; a zero-tap policy is compared with always-commit, not with a random asker at another tap rate. At high q (0.9) ignoring the partner is better than committing with context (205 vs 393 for M0) simply because the partner is then mostly wrong; it still trails the cue designs.
Cue-supplied designs (chip 21, plain word 19, M4+chip 13, chip+G1 5 per 1,000 at q=0.1) hand the system the intended polarity and are ORACLE upper bounds, not mitigations of the bare-input effect; at q=0.1 the X-frame comparison is near-definitional. Taps: the oracle arms cost the user the cue tap on every message (1,000 per 1,000).
Negation-frame gate (ask iff the remark has a negation/contrast cue): beats a frame-blind random ask at the same overall tap rate (mixed stream q=0.1: 63 vs 165 per 1,000, -102 [-110, -93]), does not beat frame-aware random ask (+2.8 [-1.4, 7.5]), loses at q=0.9 (+24 [16, 33]); the lexicon is the frame definition, so credit is minimal.
G1 on top of the plain chip, X turns: -15.4 [-21.6, -10.1] per 1,000 with final labels; -9.7 [-15.8, -4.5] with blind Claude labels on the 182 changed pairs (350 texts). Claude agrees with the judge final label on only 75% of chip texts, labelled by an author-aware single rater; call the G1 gain provisional until non-author humans label the 350 texts. SLP-weighted (x5 for negation) columns are omitted: they scale the unweighted numbers and add nothing.
Where nothing helps: no zero-tap mitigation on the bare channel is credited (M4, M5a, M5b, gate); the entropy-gated ask baseline is missing.

## 6. Critic issues and resolution
Round 1 (valid=false): defective N baseline and selection, circular C4, trivial q*, same-family authoring/judging, topic-rewriting Q, weak baselines, per-1,000 conversion on mismatched denominators. Resolved by the round-2 redesign (no-turn baseline, different authoring family, topic-locked Q, benchmark arms M0-M5, G1, word, random-ask and always-ask comparators), and per-1,000 headlines retired.
Round 2 (valid=true): C1 "equivalent" rested on an unvalidated asymmetric filter; "propositional" is not independence from the partner; author-specific C2; benchmark restates assumptions; C3 uninterpretable. Resolved: C1 leads with the unfiltered estimate and the filter is a labelled lower bound; "equivalent" removed; decomposition added (and found half-paraphrase); author subsets and writer clusters reported; C3 reworded to abstention; q grid to 0.9; oracle arms relabelled.
Round 3 (valid=true), carried as stated limits (no further compute): (M1) inconsistent comparators and q-dependent rankings, fixed here by same-tap comparisons only and removal of "where it matters"; (M2) decomposition over-read, now indicative only, no class pp; (M3) second author only on 40 families, in the headline limits; (M4) number drift untraced, current shared-code values reported, earlier ones labelled superseded; (M5) G1 gain on one rater, provisional. Unresolved: entropy-gated ask not run; human Parts A/B pending; second author on all 130 families.

## 7. Strongest TRUE claim
In a controlled setting with model-written partner remarks, feeding a negative partner remark to the keyword-to-sentence model moves bare-input output to negation in all 4 models (+36.7 pp all negation), mostly by echoing the partner's wording or agreeing with it; the excess that is not verbatim echo is +8.7 pp [6.9, 10.6] (+4.2 to +9.2 depending on the remark author, about half still a paraphrase of the partner's claim). A supplied polarity cue largely but not completely removes it (+7.05 [5.26, 9.04] counting echoes and agreements as harm; +1.2 to +1.5 pp if not). No zero-tap mitigation beats random asking at matched taps. A question remark does not increase default readings; it increases abstention. This says nothing about the rate in real AAC traffic or about whether the echoing is harmful to a user who agrees with the partner.

## 8. Novelty and closest prior work
Cai et al. 2022 (NAACL, arXiv 2205.03767; the nearest AAC context result: accuracy only, no commitment attribution, no frame manipulation; abstract figures taken from a search snippet, re-verify before citing). Related AAC partner-context work (KWickChat IUI 2022, Valencia et al. CHI 2023, COMPA CHI 2024, partner-ASR context W19-1706) reports usability, not echo or polarity-reversal measurement; COMPA's finding that the relevant context often is not the partner's last turn is the best published motivation and should be cited. General-LLM context copying and instruction-context conflict (Zhou arXiv 2407.13048, Camassa and Shiller arXiv 2605.20382, Farahani arXiv 2601.12075) and acquiescence/sycophancy work (Braun arXiv 2509.08480; the yes-bias is not the mechanism here, echo is) are general. Search 2026-11-03 found no paper that crosses partner remark with intended value and attributes echo vs silent reversal in AAC; absence is not proof. Novelty: medium. The cue-supplied result is the trivial part; the echo-attribution under a supplied cue is the AAC-specific part.

## 9. Limitations
Machine labels only (echo, agreement, class decomposition: judge-vs-lexical agreement 72-93%, decomposition judge calls 32% of affirmative controls non-independent). Two turn authors, polarity only, 130 families that are not a sample of AAC traffic, templated remarks, one greedy draw, unweighted 4-model pooling, ~20 exploratory contrasts. q unmeasured. Acceptability, fluency and real-user harm unmeasured. Echoing a remark the user agrees with may be helpful (the Cai benefit), and the bare-channel effect has no harm claim without q.
