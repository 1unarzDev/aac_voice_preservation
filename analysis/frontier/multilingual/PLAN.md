## 0. Literature check (done 2026-11-02; searched with WebSearch/WebFetch; [O]=opened, [S]=snippet only)

Closest conceptual precedent ("default as measured object, language as implicit stand-in"); not register, not AAC, not keywords. Cite as precedent, not as overlap.
- [S] Ershadi et al., "In What Languages are Generative Language Models the Most Formal?", arXiv 2302.12299: XGLM and BLOOM, 5 languages (ar, bn, en, es, fr), native speakers sorted outputs formal/informal/incohesive. Measures the formality of free continuations in base models, not chat-model defaults under an underspecified compositional task, no minimal pairs, no AAC. Must be opened before citing numbers.
- [S] Faruqui and Padó, EACL 2012 (E12-1064): T/V as NLP label (German du/Sie); background only.
- [S] Negation across languages: arXiv 2503.22395 (EMNLP Findings 2025, multilingual NLI minimal pairs), arXiv 2604.18942 (VLMs, 7 languages, gaps up to 27.5 pp). ## 1. Hypotheses (directional, falsifiable)
- H1 Register commitment: with no register cue, outputs that contain a second-person address form commit to ONE register (T or V) in >=90% of such outputs, per model x language cell (pooled >=95% is the "approaches 100%" reading). Falsified if pooled <90% or if a majority of cells are <90%.
- H2 Register direction differs by language/model: for at least one model, the V-share (formal share) differs between two languages by >=30 pp with a family-cluster bootstrap 95% CI excluding 0. Falsified otherwise (then "no language flip detected at this power"). Also report whether the majority register flips across models within a language (a model-dependent default is itself the finding that "the" default is not a language fact).
- H3 Polarity default replicates: affirmative share among committed bare polarity outputs >=90% per language pooled over models, and within 5 pp (non-inferiority margin) of English on the SAME source families (English = stored P0 bare outputs of the same models on the same hw polarity families). Falsified if any language pooled <90% with CI upper bound <90%, or if the language minus English difference has CI excluding [-5,+5] in either direction (report direction).
- H4 Cue restoration transfers: restoring the negation cue (chip rendered as a target-language word) raises P(negated) on B (negated) families by >=50 pp over bare in >=5 of 6 models in every language (lower CI bound >=50). Register cue (formal/informal chip or plain word) moves the share of the requested register to >=90%. Falsified/partial otherwise, reported per cell.
- H5 Politeness marker default (secondary): on matched please/plain families, bare outputs carry an explicit politeness marker (bitte / por favor / s'il vous plait or conditional softener) at a rate that differs from the English marker rate on the same families by >=15 pp in at least one language (flip/shift test). Exploratory.
- H6 Code-switching (exploratory, no confirmatory claim): English negation token embedded in target-language keywords ("Kaffee not") is honoured (output negated) less often than a target-language negation token ("Kaffee nicht"); effect is reported with CI, no threshold.
- Where we expect NO help (declared in advance): register has no ground truth, so a cue-less commit cannot be scored as an error without a prior; a guard that only checks listed cues cannot fire on register; chip vs plain word ~ equal (as in English, D-070).

## 2. Stimuli (draw rules fixed now; seed string "ml1")
Source = existing human-written CC BY families in data/items/hw.jsonl (hw1), so English outputs already exist for the same families.
- POL: 50 polarity families. Eligible: dimension polarity, A and B sources contain no second-person word (you/your/yours) so register is not a nuisance, bare keywords contain no negation word or please. Order by sha256("ml1|"+family_id), take first 50. Pairs: A affirmative, B negated.
- REG: 50 register families. Eligible: hw families of dimension speech_act/politeness whose A source is addressed to the listener (imperative, "can/could you", "you ..."). Order by sha256("ml1reg|"+family_id), first 50, bare keywords only (variant A bare string). True register unknown: the item is a prompt for a listener-directed message.
- PLT: 25 politeness families (please vs plain), first 25 by sha256("ml1plt|"+family_id) among those not already in REG (deduplicated by order). Bare channel only.
- CS (exploratory): 30 of the 50 POL families (first 30 by the same order), keywords code-switched in two ways: CS-en (target-language content words plus English "not"/"no"/"never" for B; nothing for A) and CS-tl (same with target-language negation word). Bare only.
- Languages: German (du/Sie), Spanish (tu/usted), French (tu/vous). Third language chosen for T-V; Japanese/Korean honorifics and Hindi are out of scope for the pilot (stated limitation; add only if H1/H2 are positive).

Translation protocol (freeze before use):
1. Forward translation of the A and B source sentences and of the bare keyword string into each language by gpt-6.1-terra (prompt: translate to {language}; keywords as lemma/infinitive forms with NO negation word and NO register-marking words; for REG keep any listener-directed verb in infinitive; do not add formal/informal pronouns). The prompt file and outputs are stored; no edits by hand.
2. Back-translation by a different generator (gpt-6.1-sol, plus a Claude Sonnet subagent for a random 20%): blind to the English original. Accept a pair iff (a) back-translation of A and B preserve polarity (judged with the existing English judge, polarity label equals the source critical value) and (b) the target bare keywords contain no negation or register marker by regex lexicon (nicht/kein/nie/nichts/ne/pas/jamais/rien/no/nunca/nada/sin; du/Sie/tu/vous/tú/usted and verb-form check) and (c) the keyword string back-translates to the English bare keywords with token overlap >=0.5 by a lemma-level match. Failing items are dropped, never repaired; if <40 POL or <40 REG pairs survive in a language, that language gets a "reduced n" label, and the stages proceed with the survivors. Report the drop table.
3. Keywords: a flagged limitation: translated keyword strings are model-produced, not what native AAC users type (no native keyword behaviour; no native abbreviation/telegraphic patterns; Spanish pro-drop and German verb-final order make "keyword" cues unlike English). The study is an audit of model defaults on translated stimuli.

## 3. Prompts and arms
- Primary prompt P0-L: the P0 template and chip legend text unchanged in English, plus one added line "Write the sentence in {German|Spanish|French}." (so legend and chip tokens stay identical; prompt-language is held fixed and the manipulation is output language). sha256 of the template, legend and added line recorded at build. New prompt ids; P0 keys untouched.
- Sensitivity prompt P0-LL: the entire template and legend translated into the target language by the same translation protocol (back-translation checked); run on bare arm only, on the 3 models in the pilot set. Tests the prompt-language confound.
- Register legend extension (declared): chips [FORMAL] and [INFORMAL] added to the legend in the register arms only, described as "address the listener formally / informally". Reported as new chips, not part of the original 19.
- Arms, polarity: bare; restored (cue chip, B: [NOT], A: [YES]), identical form to hw1 `restored`; full (typed full sentence) on B only as the loss/violation control.
- Arms, register (REG): bare; chip-formal; chip-informal; plain-word (keywords + "formal"/"informal" as ordinary words in target language); situation context ("Situation: talking to your doctor" vs "talking to your best friend", translated) as an in-context cue, to show register is instructable by content. Chips are rendered in the same fixed order as hw1.
- Arms, PLT: bare; restored ([PLEASE] / [PLAIN]).
- Generation: greedy T=0, k=1, 1 sentence, max_tokens 96 (as P0). Draw namespaces d1 (primary) and d2 (replication). Run both only on the full stage.
- Models: local qwen2.5-7b, llama3.1-8b, gemma3-4b, mistral-nemo-12b, qwen2.5-14b (one at a time under the lock) and gpt-6.1-terra via codex-lb. Pilot subset: gemma3-4b, mistral-nemo-12b, terra.

## 4. Labelling (how outcomes become labels, fixed now)
- Register (primary metric) is labelled by a deterministic detector per language (src/frontier_multilingual_register.py): T (du/dein/dir/dich/ihr(plural-T excluded by verb), tu/te/ton/ta, tú/tienes/te/tu with 2sg verb morphology), V (Sie/Ihnen/Ihr, vous/votre/vos, usted/ustedes-excluded/ su with 3sg verb), none (no second person), mixed. The lexicons are written in code and fixed before outputs; unit tests on 60 hand-built sentences per language (written by the director, not model output). Ambiguous cases (3sg verb without a pronoun, Spanish pro-drop, "ihr") go to a blind arbiter (src/arbiter_tool.py, output text only, options T/V/none/mixed). Denominator for H1/H2 = outputs with a second-person form (T, V or mixed); "none" share reported separately.
- Polarity and politeness are labelled by the existing blind judge (judge.py, J1/J2 with gpt-6.1-sol, concurrency <=3) in a language-aware wrapper: the judge sees only the target-language output sentence, with the English rubric stating "the sentence is in {language}"; route B: also judge the English back-translation of the output (gpt-6.1-sol blind) with the standard English rubric. Primary label = direct target-language judging; back-translation route is a second instrument; disagreements between instruments -> blind arbiter (output text only, target-language). No J1 fallback; unresolved rows missing, not imputed.
- Label validation (before any effect size is reported, Stage 0 gate): (i) detector vs blind Claude arbiter on a random 120 register outputs per language, agreement >=95% else the detector is revised ONCE (revision logged with pre/post numbers) and re-validated on a fresh 120; (ii) judge direct vs back-translation route on 150 polarity outputs per language, kappa >=0.85 else polarity numbers are labelled "unvalidated". (iii) Human check (provisional until returned): ANNOTATION_REQUEST.md.
- Added-content, loss and violation attribution are computed with attribute.py logic only on polarity (restored and full arms); register uses a simplified attribution: bare-arm commitment = assumption; chip-arm mismatch = violation.

## 5. Experiments (staged; each <= ~1 day compute)
- **Stage 0, cheap pilot (about 3-4 h incl. API):** 20 POL + 20 REG families, DE/ES/FR, bare arm only plus the REG chip arms, 3 models (gemma3-4b, mistral-nemo-12b, terra), P0-L and P0-LL, draw d1. Requests about 20x3x3x(1+... ) ~ 1,500 generations, ~700 judged outputs. Purpose: pipeline, translation drop rate, detector and judge validation gates. Pilot outcomes are NOT pooled into the full test; the pilot items are INCLUDED in Stage 1 (items fixed), pilot outputs kept as the d1 draw only for the pilot models, otherwise regenerated. Human annotation requested in parallel.
  - Go/No-go (fixed): GO if >=70% of pairs survive translation checks in each language AND detector validation >=95% AND judge route kappa >=0.85 in at least 2 of 3 languages. Language failing the gate is dropped from confirmatory analysis and labelled exploratory. NO-GO overall (stop, report translation-quality limits) if <2 languages pass.
- **Stage 1, full test (about 1 day):** all items, 3 languages, 6 models, arms in Sec. 3, draws d1+d2. Counts per model per language: POL 3 requests per family (bare, 2 restored) plus 1 full (B) = 200; REG 50 x 5 arms = 250; PLT 25 x 3 = 75; CS 30 x 2 = 60 => ~585 per model-language; x3 x6 = about 10.5k generations per draw. Local models ~15-25 min each per draw under the lock; terra at concurrency 4. Judge only polarity, politeness and CS outputs (about 4.5k per draw, batched calls as judge.py) at concurrency <=3; register is detector + arbiter (about 5% of rows). Expected API time 6-10 h.
- **Stage 2, baselines and policies (offline, same items, about 0.5 day, no new generation except the guard arm):**
  - Interventions and baselines on the SAME items: always-commit (default bare output), always-ask (cue chip supplied every time), random-ask (probability p matched to guard ask rate; 1,000 random draws), cue chip, plain word, context cue (register only), and the existing G1 guard (monitor + regenerate with feedback) applied to polarity (G1 needs a cue list: the human-supplied cue is absent in bare, so we report G1 as the pre-declared "does not fire" result and add G1 with the written cue to the restored arm as an upper bound, honest label). Closest published approach we implement: formality-controlled MT style explicit attribute control (the plain-word/chip register arm, after IWSLT formality track) and ask-when-uncertain via sampled-reading disagreement (INTENT-SIM style, entropy over 5 T=0.7 samples; run only for gemma3-4b and nemo on 20% of items to bound cost): ask iff the T/V (or aff/neg) vote is split.
  - Ask policy for register: ask iff output contains a second-person form (rule-based trigger, "ask when address form needed"); compared against random-ask and entropy-ask on taps per 1,000 and on mismatch.
- **Stage 3 (conditional, only if Stage 1 passes H1 and H4):** Japanese keigo or Hindi tum/aap, or a 2-model replication on one more family of models. Not planned in detail now.

## 6. Endpoints in AAC terms (formulas fixed now)
- Silent polarity reversals per 1,000 messages: for B families, P(output affirmative | bare) x pi_neg x 1000 where pi_neg is the English human-keyword prior used in D-066 (24 per 1,000 baseline for English: 2.7% negation after dropping x 0.99 affirmative default, as in analysis/robust/t2_posterior); we recompute the same quantity per language with the same pi (translation preserves pi by construction; stated assumption) and report a sensitivity grid pi in {0.5%, 2.7%, 10%}.
- Register mismatch per 1,000 messages needs a prior pi_V = P(listener needs V) which we do NOT have; report a grid pi_V in {0.1,...,0.9} and the break-even prior at which the model's default minimises silent mismatch. No single headline "error" number for register; the headline is commitment share plus the break-even analysis.
- Clinically weighted: weight by SLP severity weights for polarity and politeness from expert_package (slp analysis plan/returns, read at analysis time; the weights are fixed by that earlier plan). Register mismatch is mapped to the politeness-dimension weight, flagged as an approximation (no SLP rating on register).
- Taps per 1,000: always-ask = 1 chip tap per message; guard/ask policies = ask rate x 1 tap; plain word = 2-3 keystroke-equivalents (word typed) as in earlier D-070 accounting. Acceptability: judged fluency/naturalness of outputs by blind judge (1-5, target language) for chip vs bare, and for human acceptability via the annotation request (provisional).
- Effect sizes: paired differences on the same families with family-cluster bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples, seed 20261102); model-level and language-level cells; Holm correction across the pre-declared confirmatory tests (H1-H4: 3 languages x 6 models cells reported, primary inference on language pools); heterogeneity across models reported, no pooling hides a failing model.

## 7. Decision rules and success criteria (all fixed now)
- Overall success ("impressive but honest" bar): H1 holds in >=2 of 3 languages AND at least one of H2 or H3-difference (language effect vs English with CI excluding 0 and |diff|>=10 pp) is shown AND H4 is supported in >=2 languages. Then the frontier is promoted to a journal section/short paper with the full 6-model data and a native-speaker validation.
- Partial: H1 holds but H2 and H3 show no language effect: write "the English default is not language-specific; register is a new always-commit dimension" (still publishable as a robustness result), with the null reported.
- Negative: H1 fails in >=2 languages (models commit to impersonal/neutral phrasing or split T/V) -> report that register is not silently assumed as hypothesised, with the "none" rate; do not promote.
- Any language whose label validation (Sec. 4) fails is reported as exploratory. Any finding contingent on the translation's own bias (back-translation drop >30% for a given dimension) is flagged.
- No outcome-based change: items, arms, models, thresholds, lexicons and arbiters are frozen; deviations go to analysis/frontier/multilingual/DEVIATIONS.md with date and reason; draw d2 cannot overturn d1 verdicts but disagreements are reported.
- Not claimed: native-speaker keyword behaviour; real AAC use; that V (or T) is wrong in any case; human validation (only machine panels until the annotation returns).

**Verdict as it stands (pre-declared Sec. 7 rules applied honestly):**
- H1 holds descriptively (94/94 single-register) but is conditioned on outputs containing an address form (only ~43-58% do) and is near-tautological for one-sentence outputs. Reframed as an unconditional split: P(no address form), P(single register | address), P(mixed). Not counted as a headline.

### R2.1 Endpoints and decision rules (all computed with family-cluster bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, seed 20261102; a family resampled with all its models/arms; exact Clopper-Pearson reported where the bootstrap collapses)
- E1 (H1, unconditional): per language and model and pooled over the 6 models: P(no address form), P(single register | address form), P(mixed). No pass/fail threshold; reported as-is.
- E2 (H2, per model, Holm): V-share among address-bearing bare outputs; contrasts DE-FR, DE-ES, ES-FR per model (6 models x 3 contrasts = 18 tests; ES contrasts only if the ES detector clears the E7 gate, else the confirmatory family is DE-FR x 6 models = 6 tests). Two-sided bootstrap p = 2*min(P*(d<=0), P*(d>=0)); Holm over the family; "language effect" in a model iff Holm-adjusted p<0.05 and |d|>=30 pp. Model dependence of the register default: within a language the range of V-share across the 6 models is >=30 pp with bootstrap CI of the range excluding 0, in DE or FR.
- E3 (H3, polarity default, matched English): P(affirmative | bare, committed) per language/model, greedy AND sampled (5 draws, all draws pooled per family), minus the same on English (same 20 families, same models, same template), cluster bootstrap over the 20 families. "Language effect on polarity" iff |diff|>=10 pp and the CI excludes 0 for the sampled pool in at least one language. Also report P(negated | bare) which is the complement for silent reversals.
- E3b (register commitment vs English): P(pronoun-address) per language (target language v1 pronoun detector != none, so imperatives count as none for this comparison) vs English P(contains you/your/yours/yourself). "Language effect on commitment" iff |diff|>=15 pp with CI excluding 0 pooled over models.
- E4 (H4): restored-minus-bare negated rate on B families: per model x language lower CI bound >=50 pp. "H4 replicates on 6 models" iff >=5 of 6 (pre-declared) in EVERY language (DE, ES, FR) and the same in English. Also the plain_neg arm vs chip (chip-vs-plain-word equivalence is reported, not assumed). Negated-class agreement of the two polarity instruments (direct vs translate-then-English) on the restored B outputs: positive agreement on negated, 2*n11/(n1.+n.1), and on affirmative for A.
- E5 (register cues): match share among address-bearing outputs and address rate, for chip vs plain vs ctx, AND under the legend-equalised arms (chip - plain_x, chip - ctx_x paired on family/model). FR-informal chip failure analysis: classification of outputs by model (T, V, none) and the trigger tokens (vous/votre/s'il vous plait/veuillez) in V outputs.
- E6 (register-confound control): V-share per language x model under keyword conditions inf (pilot/terra-inf), nom, sep (DE), kw2 (second translator). The model dependence of the default "survives the control" iff, under nom keywords, the cross-model V-share range is still >=30 pp in DE or FR (CI excl 0). Translator effect = V-share(kw2) - V-share(terra-inf) per model.
