## Headline (strongest true claims, with what they do not show)
1. **The [NOT] chip result is a mechanism check, not a discovery.** Bare keywords carry no negation cue, so the models write the affirmative reading (0% negated in DE/ES, EN, 2% in FR) and the "silent reversal rate" of always-commit equals pi_neg x 1,000 = 27.0 per 1,000 by construction. Restoring the cue gives 0.0 (DE, ES, FR) and 1.4 (EN) per 1,000 pooled. This is the known positivity bias of keyword-to-sentence generation (Chen et al., ACL 2023, arXiv 2305.05976, abstract opened: LLMs "speak too positively", fail to generate negative-knowledge sentences from keywords while answering yes/no questions correctly). Our contribution is only that it holds in DE/ES/FR and in AAC units, at the same size, in these two models.
2. **At equal cost the chip and the plain word "not" are statistically indistinguishable for polarity.** Chip charged on cued messages only (27 taps per 1,000 at pi_neg = 2.7%) vs plain word 1 tap on cued messages (27 taps): negated-rate difference 2.5 pp (CI -5 to 10, EN), 2.5 (CI 0 to 7.5, DE), 7.9 (CI 0 to 17.5, ES), 2.5 (CI 0 to 7.5, FR). Silent reversals per 1,000: chip 0.0 to 1.4, plain 0.7 to 2.1. The earlier "3,000 taps" for the plain word was a cost-accounting choice; charged fairly the advantage shrinks to at most about 2 reversals per 1,000.
3. **For register the chip does beat the plain word at equal cost in ES and FR, not in DE.** Failure rates (output not in the requested register; no-address output counted as a failure; pi_V = .5; legend-equalised arms): chip 8.8 / 10.0 / 17.5% vs plain word 16.2 / 27.5 / 31.2% (DE / ES / FR), paired plain-minus-chip 7.5 pp (CI -2.5 to 17.5, DE: indistinguishable), 17.5 (7.5 to 28.7, ES, exploratory), 13.8 (5.0 to 22.5, FR). A leak-aware plain word is worse still: 7.5 / 15 / 12.5% of plain-word outputs echo the cue word ("formell"), and rejecting them costs a retry; plain-minus-chip with the output filter 10.0 (0 to 20.0), 21.2 (10.0 to 33.8), 17.5 (7.5 to 28.7). (Lower bound for the plain word: the rejected outputs were not regenerated.) The chip is a formality-attribute control in the sense of the CoCoA-MT / IWSLT 2022 line; it is not novel.
4. **The register default is model-dependent in this 2-model pilot.** French V share among address-bearing bare outputs: nemo 77.8% (7/9, Clopper-Pearson 40.0 to 97.2) vs terra 8.3% (1/12, CP 0.2 to 38.5); German nemo 45.5% (5/11, CP 16.7 to 76.6) vs terra 7.1% (1/14, CP 0.2 to 33.9). The within-language range across the two models is 38.3 pp (DE, CI 4.3 to 75.0) and 69.4 pp (FR, CI 33.3 to 100.0; the upper bound is at 100). No language effect survives Holm: the family actually used is 2 tests (DE-FR for nemo and terra; ES contrasts excluded because the ES detector failed its gate), Holm p = 0.113 (nemo, -32.3 pp, CI -66.7 to 0.0) and 1.000 (terra). The declared family (up to 18 tests) was not reached. With 2 models this is a contrast, not a heterogeneity estimate. **Spanish V share (0 of 25) is excluded from the headline** (detector 94.2% < 95% gate; 7 of 67 'none' outputs were address forms; no further tuning).
5. **Commitment (E1)**: given an address form every bare output uses a single register: DE 25/25 (Clopper-Pearson 86.3 to 100), ES 25/25 (86.3 to 100, exploratory), FR 21/21 (83.9 to 100); but no address form at all in 37.5% (DE, ES) and 47.5% (FR) of 40 bare outputs each, so "always commits" is conditional.

## What the round-3 instructions changed, item by item
**E3b (declared rule, applied literally).** Pronoun-address share vs English "you", pooled over the 2 models: DE +15.0 pp (CI 0.0 to 30.0), ES +20.0 (2.5 to 37.5), FR +17.5 (0.0 to 35.0). By the declared rule (|d| >= 15 pp and CI excluding 0) **ES meets it; DE and FR do not**. The post-hoc clean pronoun-only variant gives DE +15.0 (0 to 30), ES **-7.5 (-15 to 0)**, FR +17.5 (0 to 35): ES flips sign. Conclusion: the formal R2.9 promotion trigger (a commitment difference to English) is acknowledged as met in ES, but it rests on an unstable measure (the v1 lexicons count a few imperative/verb tokens, and Spanish is pro-drop and exploratory), so no robust commitment claim. Not promoted on this evidence.

**Non-ceiling polarity test (cheap, post-hoc added, partly failed).** Terra only (nemo's round-3 arms need the GPU; the 4 other models are pending); n = 18-20 families per language; labels by sol direct and translate-then-English route.
- `ctxq` (a yes/no question context generated by terra, regex-checked for no negation): bare outputs are negated 0.0% (CI 0 to 0) in EN, DE, ES, FR, so the default is still at the ceiling; furthermore the bare output often just echoes the question ("Fragst du die Pflegekraft nach der Rampe?"), so this arm does not measure a polarity default. It is reported as a failed non-ceiling attempt.
- `ctxn` (same context plus "The user is answering with a refusal.", added after seeing the ctxq result): bare outputs are negated 100% in all four languages (the context fully determines the default; diff to English 0.0 pp, CI 0 to 0). The **[YES] chip against that context** is honoured only 80.0% (EN, 19 families), 43.8% (DE, n=20), 35.3% (ES, n=19), 40.0% (FR, n=18) of the time: when cue and context conflict the model follows the context more often in DE/ES/FR than in English (examples, terra: DE `[YES] Pflegekraft Rampe` + refusal -> "Nein, ich frage die Pflegekraft nicht nach der Rampe."; FR -> "Oui, je refuse de demander ..."; EN -> "Yes, but I don't want to ask the nurse about the ramp."). Caveats: the conflict is artificial (the context says refusal while the chip says yes, so a lower compliance is not obviously an error), one model, English instruction frame with target-language keywords, no CI computed on this contrast as a language effect yet (the per-language gaps were not tested), no native check. It shows the default can move off 0% under a prior; it is not evidence of a language effect on the unprompted default. Reversal accounting does not apply to ctxn (the context states the polarity).

**Matched English polarity ("no language effect") is a ceiling result** (0% or about 0% negated in every language in round 2), uninformative on its own; the ctxq/ctxn arms above were the attempt to leave the ceiling.

**Register policy table = MECHANISM ILLUSTRATION** (pi_V assumed, the chip treated as ground truth for the requested register, borrowed 0.5 SLP weight). With no-address outputs counted as cost (pi_V = .5, per 1,000 messages; taps per 1,000 when every message is cued): DE commit 688, chip 88 (1,000 taps), plain 162, ctx 375, ask-if-address 375 (625 taps); ES (exploratory) 688 / 100 / 275 / 488 / 388 (625); FR 738 / 175 / 312 / 462 / 512 (525). Under the declared "no-address harmless" counting ask-if-address looked perfect (0 / 0 / 38): once a no-address output counts as a cost it leaves 375-512 per 1,000, which removes its apparent advantage over the chip; it is cheap only because it asks less. Break-even tap cost c* (mismatches avoided per tap, no-address as cost, pi_V = .5): chip 0.60 (CI 0.50 to 0.70, DE), 0.59 (ES), 0.56 (FR); ask-if-address 0.50 / 0.48 / 0.43; plain word 0.53 / 0.41 / 0.42. In words: one tap is worth it only if it is cheaper than about half a mismatch; the full pi_V grid is in `r3_c_register_policies.csv` and `summary_r3.json`. Where it does not help: French-informal chip failures remain (chip 17.5% failure in FR), and every ask policy is only as good as the assumption that the chip equals the user's intent.

**Fair costs (item 4), polarity** (per 1,000 messages, pi_neg = 2.7%, SLP NEG weight 1.0, pooled; table B in `r3_auto_tables.md`): always-commit 27.0 reversals (0 taps); chip every message 0.0-1.4 (1,000 taps); chip cued-only 0.0-1.4 (27 taps); plain 'not' 1 tap cued-only 0.7-2.1 (27 taps); plain 'not' 3 keystroke-equivalents cued-only (81) or every message (3,000, the round-2 convention). Sampled-vote ask baseline (own implementation, formerly mislabelled INTENT-SIM; that citation is dropped as unverified): asks almost never because all 5 draws agree and the model is confidently wrong, so it removes 0-1.3 of 27 reversals (round 2 numbers, unchanged).

**Leak-filter plain-word baseline (item 7).** (i) The keyword-lexicon filter removed 0 of 20 families in every language (all keyword strings already contain no register/negation lexicon), so the "filtered keywords" comparison is identical to the full one; (ii) the output filter worsens the plain word as shown above (see Headline 3). No generation-level retry arm was run.

**Control keyword meaning check (item 6; E8-style, sol and terra back-translation, overlap >= 0.5 under both; machine only, lenient).** Survivors: nominal 18/20 (DE), 17/20 (ES), 15/20 (FR); DE separable-prefix 16/16; second-translator kw2 20/20 (DE), 20/20 (ES), 19/19 (FR). On survivors only, V share (pooled, n of T/V outputs): DE nominal 20% (n=5), ES nominal 0% (n=5), FR nominal 75% (n=4); DE separable-prefix 16.7% (n=24, vs 26.1% on the same families bare, n=23); DE kw2 17.6% (n=17) vs 24.0% bare; FR kw2 42.1% (n=19, CI 23.1 to 61.1) vs 38.1% bare; ES kw2 0% (n=21). The nominal condition has almost no address forms (13-15%), so it has no power. The separable-prefix result is described only: I make no claim that it removes the German gap (n = 12/12 per model, and the overlap gate passed every kw2 string, including ones previously read as poor, so survival is weak evidence of correctness). French kw2 (a different translator) reproduces the pilot level, which argues against a translator artefact for French; German is unresolved.

**Arbiters and detector (item 5).** Sol is the sole primary arbiter for terra outputs (the one ambiguous ES output went to sol); terra-vs-sol agreement is a secondary check only, and terra is both a tested model and an arbiter, so label independence is limited; human labels not returned (ANNOTATION_REQUEST.md extended, unsent). The v2 detector revision is in-sample for the first sample; the fresh E7 validation (DE 97.5%, ES 94.2% FAIL, FR 100%) is unchanged.

## Benchmarks used (same items)
Always-commit, always-ask (chip), plain word (1 tap / 3 keystrokes; legend-equalised; with output leak filter), context cue, G1 guard (with a written cue: repaired every output, but needs the cue written first, so it is the chip plus a verification step), sampled-vote ask baseline, random-ask matched rates (round 2), ask-if-address. Closest published approaches: CoCoA-MT and the IWSLT 2022 formality track (formality-attribute control, the chip's family; not re-implemented, cited only from abstracts and search results), Chen et al. 2023 (the polarity phenomenon). Literature: Chen et al. is opened; Ersoy et al. (arXiv 2302.12299, spelled Ershadi in the brief) abstract opened; INTENT-SIM not cited.

## Stop rule (R2.9) and criteria
With 2 of 6 models the stop rule is not evaluated and no 6-model claim is made. For the record: H4 (restoration gain lower bound >= 50 pp) holds in 2 of 2 models in every language and English (round 2); E2 range criterion is met at 2 models in FR (69.4 pp) and DE (38.3 pp) but is not a 6-model range. The promote trigger via E3b (ES) is formally met but unstable (above); via E3 (polarity) not met. Expected framing if the remaining models do not change it: a model-dependent register default (pilot), the [NOT] chip as a mechanism check indistinguishable from a 1-tap plain word for polarity, and a modest equal-cost chip advantage for register in ES/FR.
