## Major issues
1. **E3b verdict overrides the declared rule, and the stop rule then reads differently.** PLAN E3b says "Language effect on commitment iff |diff|>=15 pp with CI excluding 0 pooled". The PLAN states no ES exemption for E3b; the exemption applies only to the E2 family. The declared pooled ES row is +20 pp, CI [2.5, 37.5], effect_ge15pp_ci_excl0 = True (r2_e3b_commitment_vs_english.csv). Terra alone also meets it for DE and ES (+20, CI [5, 40]). PLAN R2.9 says "if ... E3b shows an effect then PROMOTE". round2.md instead says "no robust commitment effect" and "promotion trigger not met". It gets there by switching to a clean pronoun-only variant (DEVIATIONS 15) that was added after the first table was seen. That variant flips the ES sign to -7.5 pp. A sign flip means the measure is unstable, not that the null holds. Report the declared verdict literally ("rule met in ES, exploratory; not robust to measure"). Then let the director decide. Do not write "not met".
2. **The headline polarity result (2) is a tautology.** The bare B arm gives keywords that contain no negation cue, and the "restored" arm injects the [NOT] token. "Bare negated 0%, chip 100%, gain >=88 pp" shows the model follows an explicit instruction. It does not show a default. "Always-commit reversal prob = 1.0" is by construction for B items, so 27.0 per 1,000 is just pi_neg x 1000. Entropy-ask "cannot detect default bias" is the same point, and it applies to any sampling-based baseline when the information is absent from the input. The honest claim is "negation is not recoverable from keywords, so a cue is required". That is a statement about information content, not model behaviour. It also makes the matched-English "no language effect, at ceiling" (3) uninformative: a ceiling of 100% affirmative cannot discriminate languages.
3. **The register policy table is circular.** pi_V = 0.5 is assumed because there is no ground-truth prior. The chip and G1 "0 mismatches" follow because the user's stated register is ground truth. Ask-if-address "0" is also by construction: it asks exactly when an address form exists, and a mismatch needs one. "Beats random-ask by 11.7 pp" is closed-form (address rate x 0.5 x (1 - ask rate)). Bootstrap CIs only quantify the address rate. It also scores no-address outputs as harmless, which the text admits flatters the policy. The register weight is borrowed (0.5), so the SLP-weighted register numbers have no clinical basis. Report them as a mechanism illustration, not an AAC outcome.
4. **Weak or unfair AAC-cost baselines.** The plain word is charged 3 keystroke-equivalents (3,000 taps/1,000). In symbol and core-vocabulary AAC, "not/no" is normally a single core-word tap, so the plain-word arm should be 1 tap. At equal cost the chip's advantage (0.7 vs 1.4 reversals, and 0.0 vs 0.7) is not distinguishable (chip minus plain CI includes 0 in EN and DE). The "always-ask chip" is charged 1,000 taps per 1,000 messages, while the user only needs the cue on the 2.7% negated messages (user-initiated). The tap axis therefore mixes prompt-driven and user-initiated costs. No acceptability trade-off is shown for the chip, only a machine proxy.
5. **Instrument independence and validation gaps.**
   - Arbiter 2 (terra) is also a tested model family, so 'arb1 vs arb2 = 100%' is weak evidence of validity.
   - Polarity labelling has the same sol labeller on both routes. Perfect agreement (1.000) is uninformative, as the draft says.
   - E7 was drawn from 2 models, not all round-2 outputs (DEVIATIONS 17). It had 0 detector-ambiguous cases in 360, so the "ambiguous included" requirement is not exercised.
   - ES failed the gate: 94.2% (Wilson lower bound 88.4%), and 7 of 67 'none' outputs were address forms. The ES "V = 0 of 25" figure is therefore biased low and must not appear in headline text, including headline 1.
   - The detector was revised once, and the revision tested in-sample (DEVIATIONS 10).
   - Also, the E7 gate "primary agreement" excludes ambiguous outputs from its denominator, but none occurred.
6. **E1 "commitment" is partly definitional.** "Single register given address = 100%" cannot be anything else when a single output is one sentence with one form (mixed = 0 is nearly impossible). The informative quantity is the 38-48% of outputs with no address form, which the draft notes. The "always commits" framing should be dropped.
7. **Inference at tiny n.** The Holm family was pre-declared as 6 models x contrasts. It is now 2 tests and so is not the plan's family. The V-share is conditional on address-bearing outputs, which gives n = 9-14 per cell, and the bootstrap over families with n = 9 is unreliable. The nemo DE-FR difference (p = 0.057 raw, 0.11 Holm) is an observation, not a result. "Model range FR 69 pp (CI 33-100)" has an upper bound of exactly 100, which is a bootstrap-collapse symptom. The E6 reading that separable-prefix verbs "remove" the DE model gap rests on n = 12 and 12. The declared 6-model "survives the control" rule is not evaluated, so "partly an artefact" is a hypothesis.
8. **Same-item, same-model dependence.** The ES/DE/FR cells reuse the same 20 families and the same 2 models, and the sampled draws are pooled per family. Counts such as "n = 200/200/185/196" are not independent samples, and they should not be read as such even though the bootstrap clusters on families. Every claim is about 20 translated templates, not about languages. The translations are machine-made with a back-translation gate. No native-speaker check has been done.
9. **Spanish naturalness and second-translator control.** The nemo second translator was not meaning-checked (DEVIATIONS 19). The nom/kw2 conditions therefore confound register with meaning error, and "French unchanged under a second translator" is weaker than stated.
10. **Cue benchmark confounds.** After equalising the legend, the chip still has a different token form than the plain word, and nothing controls length or position. The chip wins by 5-30 pp with CIs touching 0 in 4 of 6 cells (DE formal and informal, FR formal and informal). Say "not distinguishable from a plain word in most cells". The plain word's leakage into the output is real, but a leak filter is trivial and was not tried. The machine naturalness drop is partly leakage.

## Novelty
- The negation finding is already in the literature. Chen et al., ACL 2023, "Say What You Mean! LLMs Speak Too Positively about Negative Commonsense Knowledge" (arXiv 2305.05976), shows keyword-to-sentence generation (the CG task) drifts positive, with weak negation and unfaithful added content. The draft cites this elsewhere, but round2.md's Literature section does not. It should state that the English default is a replication and that the multilingual contribution is the invariance, which is at ceiling and so weak.
- Register default: Ersoy et al. (arXiv 2302.12299) is already cited. The T/V-in-English prior work (Faruqui and Pado, EACL 2012) and formality-controlled MT (CoCoA-MT, IWSLT 2022) are cited as controls. The attribute-chip contribution is incremental by the draft's own account.
- "INTENT-SIM" is named as the entropy-ask source, but no verified citation appears in the file. Verify it or call it "our sampled-vote baseline". I found no AAC paper on T/V or negation loss in my search, which supports a narrow novelty claim: the audit design and the AAC-unit scoring.

## Honest strongest framing
Cue-less keywords cannot carry negation, and a one-tap cue restores it in DE/ES/FR/EN for two models. The register default is model-dependent: FR 78% vs 8% V between two models, and DE is partly a keyword artefact. Self-consistency cannot detect either. Language-level claims are out of reach (20 templates, 2 models, ES instrument failed).
