# Item verification, round 2: W-C speech act (r2_c2) and W-D factorial (r2_d)

Independent verifier, 2026-10-31. I read every family by hand against PREREG_R2, SPEC v0.1, the CODEBOOK (v0.2 + CB-1..CB-9), D-016..D-019, and
the problem classes in items_r1.md. No LLM APIs or GPU servers were used. The source of truth is the builders
(src/authoring/build_r2_c2.py, src/authoring/build_r2_d.py); both JSONL files were regenerated from them.

There are no DROPs, so no family carries `dropped`. `src/build_requests.py` did not support skipping, so I added a backward-compatible skip:
`load_families` skips any record with `"dropped": true` (logging `drop_reason` to stderr), and records without the field are unaffected.
I tested it on a temporary copy with one dropped family: 79 families built, and the request count fell by exactly that family. Default and r2/r2d
builds are unchanged: r2_c2 gives 15528 unique requests; r2_d gives 5760 = 80 scenarios x 18 inputs x 4 models.

## Most common problems

1. **Cluster IDs inconsistent across strata (r2_c2, 4 errors + design).** Seeded clusters were `seed-L<line>`, while r2_c1 uses `r2c-seed-<05d>`. Four seed lines
   (2672, 3595, 2640, 1408) are used in both strata, so a pooled seed-cluster bootstrap would treat one seed as two independent clusters.
   Drafted clusters were order-sensitive templates, so `did-you~imperative` and `imperative~did-you` were split into separate clusters, and the two
   "I need you to" adversarial families sat in different clusters.
2. **Homographic past "you" statements (r2_c2 x2, r2_d x1).** Verbs whose past equals the base form (put, reset) make "You put ..." / "You reset ..."
   readable as a subject imperative or a habitual. In r2_c2 this risks a false Violation on the *critical* dimension. In r2_d it risks a speech-act
   change in a "statement" cell. All three are moved to unambiguous forms.
3. **D-018 exclusion not machine-readable.** r2act-078/079 ("I need you to ...") are keyed request/command. Applied literally, the CODEBOOK
   statement rule ("I need X") contradicts that key, so a blind annotator will not reliably reproduce it. D-018 already excludes them from P1, but
   nothing in the data or code recorded this. They are now flagged with `"primary_exclude": "D-018"`. **analyze.py must drop rows with this field
   from P1** (it does not read the field yet).

Cross-cutting observations (no change needed):
- Minimality holds for polarity (all affirmative), agent (constant within every family; I checked each one), and politeness (no please/could/would/
  thanks anywhere; "Can you" = plain per CB-2). Certainty differs between statement (plain) and question/request (n/a) by codebook definition, so
  this is inherent to SQ/RS contrasts. QR pairs are fully minimal on all general dimensions. Time co-varies in RS (request future vs past statement)
  and in "did-you vs can-you" QR. Time is unscored.
- all_chips match the sources: [THEY] for inanimate/third-person subjects, including "my phone/device" (CB-4: not body parts); no actor chip for dummy-there and
  agentless passives (002, 008, 010); [WE] for "we need/are out of". [PLAIN] appears only on request variants, as in test act-*, so chipall is one chip longer for
  requests.
- Every bare input admits both readings. In RS families the "You <past> ..." statement is the weaker reading but genuine (report, thanks or complaint). The default
  directions noted by the author are plausible.
- Seed edits (40) match the raw corpus lines (verified by validator and by eye). Edits are minimal and documented (punctuation, capitalisation,
  "the" insertion, typo "pick pu", dialect "out the fridge", tense had->has). None of the seeds overlap W-B.
- Bare inputs repeated across files (descriptive caveat): r2act-014/015/020/046/054/079 have the same bare as an r2_c1 family, so their bare-channel
  requests are deduplicated into shared outputs. These are correlated across strata, though not across the P1 contrast. r2d-001/002/003 repeat r2_c1 bares,
  but W-D inputs always carry chips.
- r2_d: all 80 scenarios are single-predicate, use "the" objects, and are past simple or present perfect. Checked programmatically: the 4 cells differ only
  in subject (I/You) and negation (didn't/haven't); each record's critical label matches its cell; every [STATEMENT] input equals its [TELL] input with only that chip
  swapped, with identical commitments; there are 9 unique inputs per encoding per scenario; prompts are P0x only, and its legend defines [STATEMENT]. Under the CODEBOOK each
  cell is statement / plain / none, with "didn't/haven't" negated and I/You speaker/addressee. Apart from r2d-027 (fixed), none of the you-cells is a
  command or a "You'll" form. "You haven't packed the hospital bag" carries reproach, but it is declarative and so a statement under CB-1.
