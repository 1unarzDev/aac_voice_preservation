# Item verification: data/items/r2_c1.jsonl (W-C polarity + politeness), independent verifier, 2026-10-31

Scope: all 160 families (80 r2pol, 80 r2plt), which I read by hand against PREREG_R2 W-C, SPEC v0.1, D-016..D-019, CODEBOOK v0.2 and CB-1..CB-9.
I used no LLM APIs and no GPU servers. After forming my own verdicts I compared them with `verifier_r2.csv`. It has no critical-value mismatches for these items, and its comments led to VR-07 and VR-10.
For each family I checked: (1) a genuine two-way collision on `bare`; (2) minimality on the general dimensions; (3) chips and all_chips against the source;
(4) the label a literal blind annotator would give the source under the CODEBOOK and CB-1..CB-9; (5) whether the noncritical word hints at one reading; (6) seed fidelity; (7) clusters and near-duplicates.

All edits were made in `src/authoring/build_r2_c1.py` (the source of truth), and the JSONL was regenerated from it.
Validator: `python3 src/validate_items.py data/items/r2_c1.jsonl` gives **0 errors** and 1 warning
(r2pol-043, bare 'pass' matches 'passed'. This is a known heuristic false positive, because 'pass' is the lemma). Strata are unchanged: 40 seeded, 30 drafted and 10 adversarial for each dimension.
Exactly 15 records changed (all polarity). No politeness record changed.

## Main findings

1. **Politeness boundary labels hold up under a literal reading.** CODEBOOK §1 explicitly says "'Can you …?' alone is plain" and lists could you / would you / would you mind / thanks / sorry as polite.
   The can-vs-could pairs (025, 033, 034, 038, 039, 071), will-vs-would (072), the sorry pairs (056, 074, 077, 079), the thanks pairs (073, 078), embedded please (075) and the CB-9 "Can I …, please?" pairs (011, 076) therefore all receive their intended value.
   The file avoids "may I", "if possible" and "excuse me", which the general politeness list counts as polite but the critical rule does not list.
2. **Refusal leakage in polarity families (VR-02/03/09/10).** The codebook's refusal cue list includes "I won't", and declining sentences ("I'm not coming", "I can't do the dishes later") can be labeled refusal.
   Because only the negated variant carries such a cue, an output faithful to its source would get a speech-act stance mismatch that is correlated with the critical contrast.
   I fixed this with per-variant `general_spec` overrides that accept {statement, refusal}, a mechanism labels.general_spec already supports. For r2pol-036, the affirmative "I can do the dishes later" also accepts offer/suggestion.
   This only widens the general speech-act spec; critical scoring is unchanged.
3. **Noncritical words that hint at one reading (VR-01/04/05/06/14):** long email, surprise party, white shirts, long walk, small print. Each was replaced by a neutral word, and bare is unchanged.
4. **Agent spec (VR-07):** "I (don't) want to be left alone" has an unnamed doer under CB-6, so the agent is not scored (the second verifier labeled it unspecified).
5. **Seed fidelity (VR-08):** seed "Don't forget to let the cat out" has the opposite meaning from variant A "Don't let the cat out", so seed_variant is now B. I checked every other seed edit and found them faithful (reason clauses dropped, pronouns made concrete, NPIs yet/anything removed).
6. **Context bias (VR-13):** "Frost is forecast." made the bare input of r2pol-059 favour the negated reading in every channel. That contaminates P3 (bare affirmative share), so I removed the context. I judged the other 16 contexts to be neutral or to license both readings. r2pol-034's pothole context leans affirmative but is plausible as a check, so I kept it.
7. **Negative and embedded polarity.** For r2pol-029/071/072 (embedded under say/tell), a literal reading gives negated via "what it directly embeds". I marked r2pol-077 ("Isn't the museum free on Sundays?") OK with a **sensitivity note**: the words are negated, but a careful annotator could invoke "negation only shows uncertainty" and choose unclear/mixed. Report P3 with and without this family, or exclude A from the Violation analyses.
