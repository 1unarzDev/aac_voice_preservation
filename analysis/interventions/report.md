Method. **I1 commitment guard**: after the P0 generation, the deterministic layer0 extractor (+ a
number/tense add-on) compares the output with the commitments the user supplied at runtime (chips parsed
from the visible input; for `full`, layer0 labels of the typed source), and flags a polite marker that was
not supplied. On a flag, the same conversation gets one feedback turn ("Your sentence must keep: ...")
and is regenerated, up to 2 times; the returned output is the first unflagged attempt, else the fewest-flag
attempt (ties -> original). **G1s** = strip-only: deterministic removal of an unsupplied "please", no call.
**I2 ambiguity-complete set** (bare input): keep P2's top suggestion; for polarity then certainty, if
layer0 finds no slot holding the alternative reading of the top suggestion, replace slot 3 (then 2) with an
LLM rewrite carrying that reading, layer0-verified with one feedback retry. I2t rewrites the top suggestion;
I2 (primary, chosen on the pilot) rewrites the replaced slot's own sentence. Evaluation is by the blind
LLM extraction judge (gpt-6.1-sol, J1/J2), cross-run authoritative critical resolution
(arbiter > J1/J2 agreement > unknown), and deterministic attribution; the
guard never sees judge labels. CIs: family-cluster percentile bootstrap (src/analyze.family_bootstrap).
First passes (P0, P2, S3) are the cached generations of the base run.

## Caveats

1. **Monitor and evaluator are independent but correlated.** The guard uses layer0; the endpoint uses the blind LLM judge. They never share calls, but they target the same constructs, and judge.py breaks J1/J2 ties on general dimensions with layer0 (critical labels never use layer0). On the first pass, layer0 flags vs judged failures: TP 77, FP 19, FN 3, TN 1293 (kappa 0.867). On the repaired (flagged and regenerated) targets, layer0 'fixed' agrees with judge 'pass' on 95% of n=96 (kappa 0.818); see the per-dimension raw J1/J2-vs-layer0 agreement and the tie-break count in the guard section. A guard fix is therefore partly a fix 'as seen by an extractor of the same kind'; human gold on the repaired items would be the independent check.
2. **Fluency is not measured.** The extraction judge labels dimensions; it does not score grammaticality, so a repair such as 'Ask you to wear headphones.' (chip echo) counts as a strict pass. 'valid' = non-empty. A fluency/naturalness rating of repaired outputs and I2 rewrites is needed before claiming user benefit.
3. **What the guard cannot see stays broken.** Unfixed violations concentrate on speech act (refusal without a 'no' formula, '[TELL]' rendered as an imperative) where layer0 is weak or the model resists feedback; the number/tense add-on is lexical and conservative.
4. **I2 trades coverage between dimensions.** It guarantees polarity and certainty alternatives by replacing up to two P2 slots, which lowers speech-act and politeness coverage (agent too for I2t). Rewritten slots carry a stance commitment by design (a negated or hedged reading), so 'any stance addition' is high for them; hedge added on certainty rewrites is the intended effect, not an error. I2 (in place) was chosen over I2t on the pilot because it preserved agent coverage; both are reported on test.
5. **Serving deviation.** During these runs an external ollama process (user's, ~10 GB) shared the GPU, so the new guard/rewrite calls of the local models were served with partial CPU offload (NGL 14 qwen/llama, 20 gemma; parts of the pilot ran fully on GPU). First passes are the cached full-GPU generations. Local latencies are therefore upper bounds and only comparable within this report; greedy outputs are not bit-reproducible across serving setups anyway (README: 81-88% agreement).
6. **Small pilot.** Kill criteria were checked on 12 dev families (24 cue-channel violations, wide CIs) before the test run; the test split confirms them with family-cluster CIs. Bare/noncrit rows are shared by variants A and B and counted once per target, as in attribute.py.
