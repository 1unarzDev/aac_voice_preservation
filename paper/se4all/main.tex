\begin{abstract}
A message composed with AAC is spoken as the person's own, so when a language model expands a few keywords and silently loses a \say{not}, it puts words in their mouth; two speech-language pathologists rated that change the most serious of \RES{slp_ntypes} types (\RES{slp_neg} of 5 each, and both would block it).
Deciding whether an output kept the meaning needs a test oracle, an automated checker that is itself software and can be wrong, so we built a test harness with six requirements and tested its oracle with software-testing methods.
The oracle caught \kof{orr_j_mut} rule-seeded mutants of stored outputs, a circular sensitivity measure, with \kof{orr_j_fa} false alarms (\pct{oi_all} on mutants built without its lexicon); on two held-out sets (\RES{nat_ho_viol_npos} violation positives) its violation verdicts have \pct{nat_ho_viol_p} \cip{nat_ho_viol_p} precision and \pct{nat_ho_viol_r} \cip{nat_ho_viol_r} recall against human coders; its critical label equals the human consensus on \kof{tc_c} treated outputs; and non-author annotators side with its tie-breaking arbiter in \pct{p19_arb} of \RES{p19_arb_n} codings (chance is 50\%; arbiter rulings only).
A seeded-fault sweep of \RES{sf_n} mutants of the harness exposed two untested components (judge post-processing and the input builder); regression tests added after the sweep raised the share of mutants killed from \RES{sf_r1_k}/\RES{sf_den} to \RES{sf_r2_k}/\RES{sf_den}, a coverage score on a fixed list and not an independent estimate.
With the tested oracle, four models answered bare polarity keywords affirmatively in \pct{hwaff} of committed outputs on human-written pairs, and supplying the missing cue raised the intended speech act by \RES{hx_p1}\,pp over a length-matched word [\RES{hx_p1_lo}, \RES{hx_p1_hi}], writer-level $t$, \RES{hx_wr} writers, all positive, exploratory (first round alone, \RES{hx_r1_wr} writers: \RES{hx_r1}\,pp; pooling the second round was decided after seeing it); on a machine-written confirmation set it is \RES{wcp1}\,pp, \RES{wcp1_um}\,pp with post-hoc arbitrated labels masked; the patterns replicate on \RES{mb_nmod} models from \RES{mb_nfam} families.
Passing the oracle is necessary but not sufficient, and the extractor shares a model family with one generator: writers rejected \kof{wm_right_rej} (\pct{wm_right_rej}) of the oracle-passed outputs they rated, mostly for compression loss and changed event or role structure, the person, not the model, owns the message, and two paid design consultants who use AAC asked for an approval requirement (R6) and a NOT-scope clause, among other changes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% =====================================================================
Some people who use augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) compose a message from a few keywords, because each selection costs time and effort~\cite{valencia2023less,cai2024speakfaster}.
A language model then expands the keywords into a sentence~\cite{shen2022kwickchat,cai2024speakfaster}, which is spoken as the person's own words.
Typing \kw{supervisor read} might mean \say{The supervisor read} or \say{The supervisor did not read} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}).
Whether the model erred depends on what the user supplied, and a text-similarity score cannot know that.
A reversed \say{not} is a decision the person did not make: the pathologists would block it.
\listenerslot{}
Symbol- and grid-based AAC is out of scope.

This paper asks which output changes count as failures, given exactly what the user supplied, and how to test the checker that decides.
The software-engineering problem is the oracle.
Deciding whether an output kept the meaning needs a checker, here an LLM that extracts a few explicit labels.
An unvalidated checker silently decides every result, so we treat testing the oracle as a first-class contribution beside testing the models.
We contribute:
(1)~a harness with six requirements R1--R6 that have stated sources and, where the oracle can see them, executable tests (\S\ref{sec:harness});
(2)~tests of the oracle, including a human check of its arbiter, a seeded-fault kill matrix with regression tests added for the components it exposed, a re-label by a second model family, two defects we made ourselves, a production rule that passes its keyed gate with zero margin after a stricter rule was rejected, and the boundary of what the oracle checks (\S\ref{sec:oracle});
(3)~evidence on human-written pairs and on a machine-written confirmation set that the tests find real, model-specific behavior (\S\ref{sec:results});
(4)~a requirements review by two paid design consultants who use AAC (not a sample), with each change traced (\S\ref{sec:consult}); and
(5)~the harness and stored outputs as an artifact.
The harness is for developers, not for assessing people who use AAC or their messages.

\section{Requirements and Harness}
\label{sec:harness}
%% =====================================================================
\paragraph*{Families and channels}
A \emph{family} is a minimal pair of source messages that share content words and differ on one \emph{critical} dimension: polarity, agent (who acts), certainty, speech act (question, request or statement), politeness, or time/quantity.
Each variant is rendered through five \emph{channels}, which say how much of the message the user supplied: \emph{bare} keywords; \emph{+word}, which adds one noncritical content word; \emph{+chip}, which adds the critical value as a chip such as \chip{NOT}; \emph{+all}, with every chip the variant needs; and \emph{full}, the source sentence itself.
Every variant needs at least one chip, so the unmarked reading gets a counter-chip (e.g., \chip{PLAIN}).
A chip is a \emph{probe} of whether supplied information is used, not a proposed interface: its real cost depends on the access method (a dwell, a menu traversal, a grid button), and it adds a vocabulary to learn and an error-recovery path.
The tested lever is the missing cue; the chip is one encoding of it, and two controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}) score an irrelevant chip and a plain word.

\paragraph*{Requirements}
For output $y$ from input $x$, let $L_d(y)$ be the extracted value on dimension $d$ and $C(x)$ the commitments that $x$ carries.
Table~\ref{tab:trace} states each requirement with its source and its test.
A \emph{Violation} means a supplied value was not kept (R1, R2); an \emph{Assumption} means the output committed to a value the input left open (R3).
A reference-fidelity score would charge the model for what the interface omitted, and a similarity score would pass an added \say{please} on a typed sentence.
R1--R5 are our proposal, derived from the structure of the task and from defects we met; two paid design consultants then reviewed them and asked for R6 and refinements (\S\ref{sec:consult}).
The declared policy behind R2, keep typed wording unless the person asks for a rewrite and sees it first, protects authorship.
Some people prefer that a system rewrite their text, so R2 is a policy to test.

\paragraph*{Harness}
Fig.~\ref{fig:harness} shows the pipeline.
A family specification is rendered into channels, and the R5 validator checks that each displayed chip matches the claim.
A generator produces outputs.
An extractor (gpt-6.1-sol; not a generator, but from the same family as the generator gpt-6.1-terra, so self-preference~\cite{zheng2023judging} is possible, and the human checks of Section~\ref{sec:oracle} are the mitigation) answers the family's critical question and five general questions from the output alone; added-content extraction also sees the visible input.
Two extractor prompts (J1, J2) differ in wording and option order, and critical disagreements go to a cross-family arbiter (a blind Claude Sonnet 5.5 session shown only the output text and the question, which ruled after outcomes were known; the same arbiter ruled on the human-written-pair, model-breadth and confirmation-set units) or stay unknown.
Verdicts are then computed in code from labels, item specification and channel, so a rule change re-scores every stored output without model calls.
The oracle decides only whether a fixed set of explicit, text-level values is kept.
It does not judge fluency, referents or whether anyone would accept the sentence.

\paragraph*{Evidence sets}
All items are authored or crowd-written, not messages from people who use AAC.
The \emph{human-written pairs} (primary) are \RES{hw_nkept} pairs kept of \RES{hw_ncand} candidates, written by \RES{hw_nwriters} non-author writers over six dimensions and kept after three blind independent checks; a second round of speech-act pairs adds writers to that contrast only (pooled below); \RES{hw_nfam} families are analysed, because \RES{hw_nexcl} speech-act family falls in the refusal/acceptance quarantine of Defect~A.
The \emph{confirmation set} has \RES{wc_n_fam} fresh machine-written families on three dimensions, whose three contrasts were pre-specified in a git-timestamped plan frozen before generation.
The \emph{development set} has \RES{n_fam_test} authored families used to develop prompts and codebooks.
\emph{Corpus transfer} uses crowd-written fictional messages from AACText~\cite{vertanen2011imagination}.
Four models run with one fixed one-sentence prompt (greedy): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-3-4B-it, three open models run locally with 4-bit quantization, and one API model, gpt-6.1-terra. Two larger open models were added later under the same prompts (\RES{mb_nmod} models from \RES{mb_nfam} families on all arms).
Unless labelled, intervals are pointwise 95\% percentile-bootstrap intervals from writer-cluster (human-written pairs) or seed/construction-cluster (confirmation set) resamples, 10,000 over a fixed model roster; the exceptions are the writer-level $t$ intervals of Table~\ref{tab:primary}, Wilson intervals for proportions in Table~\ref{tab:oracle}, and cluster-bootstrap $\kappa$ intervals.

\section{Results: Does the Harness Find Real Behavior?}

\subsection{Primary replication on human-written pairs}
Table~\ref{tab:primary} gives the three contrasts.
\emph{Act-chip gain} is the speech-act chip minus a length-matched word.
\emph{Net politeness addition} is politeness added to plain typed sentences minus politeness removed from polite ones.
\emph{Affirmative default} is the share of committed (valid affirmative or negated) bare polarity outputs that are affirmative.
They were pre-specified for the confirmation set; on the human-written pairs they are an exploratory replication, not preregistered, and each rests on \RES{hw_wr_min}--\RES{hw_wr_max} writers.
Each contrast uses the families of its own dimension (speech act \RES{hwf_act}, politeness \RES{hwf_pz}, polarity \RES{hwf_pol} of the \RES{hw_nfam} analysed families).
Every writer's mean is positive in all three contrasts; pooling a second round of speech-act pairs gives \RES{hx_wr} writers for the act chip (\RES{hx_fams} families), leave-one-writer-out \RES{hx_loo_min} to \RES{hx_loo_max}\,pp, and one-sided sign-flip $p=\RES{hx_sf_p}$ (two-sided $\RES{hx_sf_p2}$), the floor for ten writers; politeness and polarity keep six and seven writers (one-sided floors $1/64$, $1/128$). Pooling the second round for this one contrast was a post-hoc decision; round 1 alone (\RES{hx_r1_wr} writers) gives \RES{hx_r1}\,pp \ci{hx_r1}. We claim direction, not width.
Effects vary by model for the act chip (pooled: Llama \RES{hx_llama}\,pp, whose writer means are not all positive, against \RES{hx_oth_min}\,pp or more for the other three, gpt-6.1-terra \RES{hx_terra}) and for politeness (Gemma \RES{t6p2_gemma}, Llama \RES{t6p2_llama}).
No robustness check (near-duplicates collapsed, one writer excluded, arbiter calls reversed, all candidates) flips a decision (largest shift \RES{hw_shift_max}\,pp).
\paragraph*{Controls: the cue, not the chip}
Two controls on the human-written pairs (\texttt{analysis/chip\_controls/}) show that restoring the missing cue is what fixes the default.
An irrelevant chip adds little (the cue chip beats it by \RES{cc_irrel_min} to \RES{cc_irrel_max}\,pp), and the same cue as a plain word is within \RES{cc_nl_near_max}\,pp of the chip on \RES{cc_nl_near} of \RES{cc_nl_dims} dimensions and beats it on speech act by \RES{cc_nl_act}\,pp: the chip is our probe, not a requirement.
\paragraph*{Same-items cue restoration and clinical weight}
On \RES{sc_nunits} elicited messages whose cue the typist dropped (three models, machine-judged), supplying the cue cuts expected silent polarity reversals per 1,000 messages from \RES{sc_pol_bare} to \RES{sc_pol_chip} (chip) and \RES{sc_pol_word} (word), and politeness reversals from \RES{sc_pz_bare} to \RES{sc_pz_chip} and \RES{sc_pz_word}.
Two speech-language pathologists ($n=2$, expert review, not a population estimate) rated a dropped or added negation the most serious silent change (\RES{slp_neg} of 5 each); they accepted \RES{slp_acc_rep}\% of repairs as written, against \RES{slp_acc_orig}\% of the failed originals.

\subsection{Confirmation set}
All three contrasts have the same sign on the confirmation set, and every interval excludes zero (Table~\ref{tab:primary}).
Act-chip gain (\RES{wcp1}\,pp) is a little below the human-written \RES{hx_p1}\,pp, and the Affirmative default is of similar size.
Net politeness addition is small, with a lower bound of \RES{wcp2_lo}\,pp.
Two qualifications: \RES{wc_prefreeze} cached requests predate the final item freeze, and Act-chip gain relies on post-hoc arbitration of \RES{wc_arb_rows} extractor-disagreement rows; with those labels masked as unknown it is \RES{wcp1_um}\,pp (last row of Table~\ref{tab:primary}), and the other two contrasts contain no arbitrated rows.
\say{Pre-specified and git-timestamped} means a private-repository commit before generation, not independently verifiable; the public hash release \texttt{\RES{hash_release}} post-dates the freeze and does not certify it.

\subsection{Assumptions, full-input violations and model heterogeneity}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} separates the two kinds of evidence.
In the italic cells the input is ambiguous, so the match is the model's default, bounded by a 50\% design ceiling; it is not accuracy.

\section{Testing the Oracle}
\label{sec:oracle}
%% =====================================================================
Every result above is only as good as the checker, so we test it as software, in the spirit of the test-oracle literature~\cite{weyuker1982nontestable,barr2015oracle} and of mutation analysis~\cite{jia2011mutation}: an authorized change must pass and an unauthorized one must fail.
\emph{Identity test:} an unchanged source injected as its own full-channel output must pass.
\emph{Scoped mutants:} a hand-written one-value change to a development-set source must be detected.
\emph{Human gate:} extractor labels against human coders.
A \emph{mutant} is an output copy with one value changed, a \emph{control} one with a surface edit only; \emph{keyed} means the expected outcome was fixed (hashed) before the first extractor call; J1 and J2 are the two extractor prompts and the \emph{arbiter} rules on their critical disagreements; \emph{development} items built the codebook and prompts (in-sample), \emph{held-out} sets did not.

\paragraph*{Two defects, two regression tests}
Two unseeded defects arose while building the harness; each now has a regression test.
In Defect A, the identity test failed \RES{id_fail_raw} of \RES{id_n} reproductions, all one sentence, \say{Yes, you can borrow my car this weekend.}, coded \emph{\RES{id_fail_got}} against the target \emph{\RES{id_fail_want}}: the model had done its job and the checker demanded a distinction the text need not force.
The obvious repair, accepting \emph{statement} for an acceptance target, passed identity but failed the next test: the relaxed oracle detected only \kof{or_acc_mut_det} acceptance mutants and missed both statement substitutions (\say{You borrowed my car this weekend.}), whereas without the relaxation it detected \kof{or_new_mut_det_raw} mutants across groups.
This is one sentence in one family, a demonstration and not a rate: removing a false alarm removed fault sensitivity, so both refusal/acceptance families are quarantined from every primary result (leaving \RES{n_fam_primary} of \RES{n_fam_test} development families) until a predicate passes both tests; keyed acceptance mutants now guard the rule.
In Defect B, an audit of \RES{wb_msgs_restored} restored-chip inputs built from AACText messages found \RES{wb_conflicts} whose displayed chip contradicted the stored source, e.g., \chip{MAYBE} for a plain assertion, after a silent fallback in our builder; in the \RES{wb_maybe_msgs} \chip{MAYBE} messages all \RES{wb_maybe_n} outputs followed the chip and failed the source, which a fidelity score counts as model failures.
The R5 validator decodes only the rendered input and now rejects all \RES{r5_conf} before generation.
Replayed on all \RES{t9_inputs} chip inputs of the three studies, it found \RES{t9_conflicts} conflicts; \RES{t9_unknown} are outside its decoder.

\paragraph*{Seeded faults, two untested components, new regression tests}
The two defects were found by accident, so we also seeded faults deliberately: \RES{sf_n} one-line mutants of the harness source in \RES{sf_ncls} classes (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}), each run against the whole existing test pool; a mutant is \emph{killed} if any test fails on it, by assertion or output mismatch and not by crash (\RES{sf_crash_only} kills by crash or timeout only).
Round~1 killed \RES{sf_r1_k} of \RES{sf_den} mutants (\pct{sf_r1}, Wilson \cip{sf_r1}).
The survivors were not scattered: judge post-processing (\RES{sf_k3_r1} of \RES{sf_k3_n} killed) and the input builder (\RES{sf_k4_r1} of \RES{sf_k4_n}) had no test that ran their code on stored data, so almost every seeded fault in them survived, including ones that silently relabel outputs.
We added two test files: a replay of the judge's post-processing over about 41,000 stored judgments with a golden rebuild of the stored requests, and a few hand-derived cases.
The same \RES{sf_n} mutants were re-run unchanged (same mutation-table hash): \RES{sf_r2_k} of \RES{sf_den} are killed (\pct{sf_r2}, Wilson \cip{sf_r2}), \RES{sf_r2_surv} survive, all by assertion, and the full suite passes on clean code (Table~\ref{tab:matrix}).
This is a coverage score of a fixed list, not a fault-detection rate: the new tests were written after seeing which mutants survived and what they change, \RES{sf_new} kills come from them, and the replay reads stored J1/J2 fields as the raw responses and stored layer-0 labels, not recorded API payloads.

\paragraph*{The oracle on faults nobody seeded}
Human-labelled natural outputs test the oracle on faults nobody seeded: judge verdict against human-consensus verdict, with violation and preservation failure as the positive classes.
The \emph{development items} built the codebook and extractor prompts and are in-sample; two \emph{held-out sets}, a second gold set of fresh outputs and the human-written pairs (\RES{nat_ho_nunits_viol} verdicts), were not used to build either.

\paragraph*{Necessary, not sufficient}
The oracle checks a fixed set of explicit values, so passing it is necessary for a faithful sentence, not sufficient.
In the writer-intent check, \RES{p18_nwriters} writers judged \RES{p18_acc_n} outputs for their own pairs: the cue raised the share called the intended version by \RES{p18_gain}\,pp [\RES{p18_gain_lo}, \RES{p18_gain_hi}], yet writers accepted \RES{p18_acc_k} of \RES{p18_acc_n} as written.
Of the \RES{wm_right_rej_den} oracle-correct outputs with an intended-version rating, writers said \RES{wm_right_rej_k} (\pct{wm_right_rej}) were not the version they meant.
The reasons are compression loss and misresolved events, not added content (the primary reason in \RES{wm_added_prim_k} of \RES{wm_notacc_n} rejections): \pct{wm_dropped} of the rejected outputs lack words from the writer's sentence that no keyword or chip supplies, and \pct{wm_subst} show a different event or role.
The person, not the model, owns the message: an output the oracle passes still needs the person's approval (R6), a cue the person controls, and the exact typed words kept for typed sentences (R2).
Both SLP raters asked that relations (who does what) be preserved; R4 now says so (an SLP request).
Event and role structure is a missing requirement, not a fault of the oracle; a separate test must ask whether the sentence says the same thing.
The cheapest such check, T1, requires that a \chip{TELL} input whose keywords hold no form of \kw{tell} not yield \kw{tell/tells/told/telling}; on the human-written pairs \RES{t1_k} of \RES{t1_n} such outputs (\pct{t1}; \RES{t1_fam_k} of \RES{t1_fam_n} families) do, against \pct{t1_ctrl} of same-source bare outputs.
T1 is lexical and not human-validated; the critical check passes these outputs because the speech act is a statement.

\section{Related Work}
%% =====================================================================
\paragraph*{Test oracles, metamorphic testing and requirements for ML}
Whether an output is correct is the oracle problem~\cite{barr2015oracle}; partial or pseudo oracles are standard~\cite{weyuker1982nontestable}, and metamorphic testing checks relations between runs~\cite{chen2018metamorphic}.
Mutation analysis measures a test suite by whether it detects seeded faults~\cite{jia2011mutation}; we apply it to the oracle, not the system.
Requirements engineering for ML calls for explicit, testable requirements~\cite{vogelsang2019re4ml}; R1--R6 are one such set, with sources and tests stated.

\paragraph*{Testing LLM systems}
CheckList organizes behavioral tests~\cite{ribeiro2020checklist} and Cho et al.\ catalogue metamorphic relations for LLM-based NLP~\cite{cho2025metamorphic}; we claim no new testing technique.
LLM judges show position, verbosity and self-preference biases~\cite{zheng2023judging}, and Pauli et al.\ show that preservation metrics look valid when test data confound style change with content errors~\cite{pauli2025stylegap}; our identity and mutant tests apply that idea to a checker.

\paragraph*{Preservation and LLMs in AAC}
Qu et al.\ evaluate speech-act retention in style transfer~\cite{qu2023conditioning}, and rewriting can keep semantic similarity while erasing pragmatic markers~\cite{navneet2026voice}.
AAC systems studied with users expose user-side controls over context or stance~\cite{valencia2023less,valencia2024compa}; a Lacosse et al.\ preprint keeps a short list of bracketed function words, including \say{not}~\cite{lacosse2026cognitivesteering}; Choudhury et al.\ test whether affect cues reach the output~\cite{choudhury2025alignment}.
We add a conditional specification of which commitments are testable under each input, and tests of the oracle that checks them.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
%% =====================================================================
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item \emph{Construct.} Labels are coarse, explicit categories; referents, negation scope, certainty degree and event structure are only partly covered (\S\ref{sec:oracle}), and passing the checker is weaker than keeping the typed wording.
\item \emph{Oracle.} The main limitation: all rates depend on an LLM extractor and arbiter, and the extractor shares a model family with one generator; a Claude re-label (above) answers this for the act-chip gain, but the same family also arbitrated and assisted. The defect trace has $n=2$ defects found by our own process, and the sweep's \RES{sf_r2_k}/\RES{sf_den} covers mutants that informed the new tests: both show that the process works, not that it finds defects we did not anticipate.
\item \emph{External.} \RES{mb_nmod} models from \RES{mb_nfam} families (open models at most 14B, 4-bit), one prompt, English, constructed and crowd-written rather than messages from people who use AAC; effects are model-specific, and zero observed events do not establish zero risk.
\item \emph{Use.} No outside developer has used the harness yet. The SLP raters and the design consultants are two people each and neither establishes what people who use AAC in general want; selection counts omit reading, navigation and fatigue~\cite{frisch2026complicated}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
%% =====================================================================
An oracle for meaning preservation is software and needs tests of its own.
Ours caught a repair that hid real substitutions and a builder that supplied the wrong chip, and a seeded-fault sweep (\RES{sf_r1_k} of \RES{sf_den} mutants killed) found two components with no tests, which now have them (\RES{sf_r2_k} of \RES{sf_den}, on a list that informed the tests); its critical labels agree with human coders on three sets of outputs, including all \kof{tc_c} treated outputs checked, and people side with its arbiter on \pct{p19_arb} of disputed labels.
With it, human-written pairs show that bare keywords leave a default reading, that supplying the missing cue, as a chip or a plain word, removes most expected reversals, and that some models change typed text.
The tests also mark their own edge: passing the oracle is necessary but not sufficient, because event and role structure is a requirement it does not check, and review by two design consultants and two SLP raters added R6 and relations in R4, which led to a proposed regression test (T1) for the missing structure, lexical and awaiting human validation.
Families, requirements, harness, oracle tests, stored outputs and analysis code are available at \url{https://github.com/1unarzDev/aac_voice_preservation}.

\section*{Ethics and Positionality}
No data about people who use AAC were collected; AACText is public under CC BY 4.0.

The paraphrases in Table~\ref{tab:consult} have not been member-checked.
The requirements remain our proposals, and we make no claim about benefit to people who use AAC.
We drew on writing by and with autistic and AAC-using adults~\cite{zisk2019aac,donaldson2021everyone,frisch2026complicated} and on participatory-research and double-empathy work~\cite{nicolaidis2019aaspire,milton2012double} to avoid encoding a group style or deficit.
