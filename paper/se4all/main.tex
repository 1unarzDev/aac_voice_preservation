\begin{abstract}
A person using an augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) app typed \say{Open this pickle jar for me.}; the app's LLM sentence expander returned \say{Please open this pickle jar for me.}
Whether that edit is a defect depends on what the input specified: here the person supplied the whole sentence, so the expander changed a choice it was given.
When the person selects only keywords, by contrast, words such as \say{not} or \say{please} may never reach the model, and its guess is a default, not a violation.
We turn this distinction into executable requirements that score a commitment only when the input carried it, and into a partial test oracle whose own failure modes we test.
On \RES{wc_n_fam} fresh authored families and four models, all three pre-registered contrasts held after Holm correction; for instance, fully typed plain requests gained a politeness marker in \kn{wcpzadd} outputs, polite ones lost it in \kn{wcpzrem}, with most additions from one model.
The extractor behind these scores agreed with two human annotators on held-out items at $\kappa=\RES{human_gkappa_min}$--$\RES{human_gkappa_max}$ across all seven label fields.
Testing the oracle mattered: relaxing a rule to remove false alarms also hid real substitutions, so the affected families are quarantined.
\end{abstract}

LLM keyword expansion turns such a selection into a sentence~\cite{shen2022kwickchat,cai2024speakfaster}; some systems also let the person send a sentence directly~\cite{tang2026throat}.
Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}A shows what can happen when the feature is given a sentence the person already typed in full: Gemma-3-4B added \say{Please}.
Across four models and \RES{wc_n_fam} fresh directive families, plain typed sentences gained a politeness marker in \kn{wcpzadd} outputs (\pct{wcpzadd}), and polite ones lost theirs in \kn{wcpzrem} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ratchet}).
The pattern is concentrated, not universal: Gemma added a marker in \kn{wcpzadd_gemma} and gpt-6.1-terra in \kn{wcpzadd_terra}, whereas Llama and Qwen removed markers more often than they added them.

Our preservation policy is a software requirement, not a claim about anyone's private intent.
A sentence without \say{please} may or may not reflect a deliberate choice; the requirement is only that the system not change typed wording unless the person asks for rewriting.
Adding \say{please} also changes how a message reads: politeness suggestions shift how writers phrase messages and how they perceive their social relationship~\cite{zhang2026politeness}.

The opposite case looks similar but is not a defect.
The keywords \kw{feel well} do not say whether the person feels well; Qwen wrote \say{I feel well.} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}B).
No model can be blamed for that guess, but the interface decides who must pay an extra selection to say \say{not}.
Given \kw{[NOT] feel well}, the same model wrote \say{I don't feel well.}
A developer needs to know which case a failed reference match is: a choice the input never carried calls for a better input channel; a choice the model received and changed calls for a preservation check; a fully typed sentence may need no generation at all.

We treat this distinction as an accessibility requirement that can be specified, tested and regression-checked.
We contribute
(1)~\emph{input-conditioned requirements} (\S\ref{sec:contracts}) that score a commitment only when the visible input supplied it, run over controlled input channels of minimal-pair families;
(2)~\emph{a partial oracle that is itself tested} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}), including a defect that our own repair introduced, with a passed human-agreement gate;
(3)~\emph{evidence and developer decisions} (\S\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:decisions}): pre-registered contrasts on fresh items, a model- and encoding-specific regression, a corpus transfer that exposed a defect in our own input builder, and a copy baseline for typed sentences.
We ran no study with AAC users and make no claim about benefit, effort or preference.

\section{Related Work}
%% =====================================================================
\paragraph*{Testing LLM systems}
CheckList organizes behavioral tests into minimum-functionality, invariance and directional tests~\cite{ribeiro2020checklist}; Cho et al.\ catalogue metamorphic relations for LLM-based NLP~\cite{cho2025metamorphic}; COLLIE tests compositional generation constraints~\cite{yao2024collie}.
Sensei combines a user simulator with a DSL for assertions and metamorphic relations~\cite{delara2026sensei}.
Extracting fields, asserting properties and relating test cases are therefore established; we claim no new testing technique.
LLM judges show position, verbosity and self-preference biases~\cite{zheng2023judging,panickssery2024selfpreference}, similarity metrics can miss negation~\cite{koch2022selfeval}, and LLM politeness judgments agree with each other more than with humans~\cite{wang2026politemisaligned}.
We therefore restrict the judge to extraction of explicit markers and test it.
Constraint order affects multi-constraint instruction following~\cite{zeng2025order}, which bears on our composition result.

\paragraph*{Preservation during rewriting}
Qu et al.\ condition style transfer on the source dialog act and evaluate act retention~\cite{qu2023conditioning}; preserving a speech act while rewriting is thus not new.
LLMs favor particular politeness strategies in free production~\cite{zhao2025politeness}, and rewriting can keep semantic similarity while erasing pragmatic markers, which preservation prompts reduce but do not remove~\cite{navneet2026voice}.
Our focus is narrower: whether a marker the person supplied, or explicitly did not supply, survives.

\paragraph*{LLMs in AAC}
Systems studied with AAC users~\cite{valencia2023less} expose intent controls such as intent buttons~\cite{valencia2024compa}, intention choices for people with aphasia~\cite{mao2025designprobes} and stance tags~\cite{xiang2025socializechat}.
Lacosse et al.\ keep bracketed function words such as \say{not}~\cite{lacosse2026cognitivesteering}; Choudhury et al.\ test whether affect cues reach the output~\cite{choudhury2025alignment}; Gorenshtein et al.\ benchmark intent drift under decoder errors~\cite{gorenshtein2026intentdrift}; R-SPEAK was co-designed with people with aphasia and scores faithfulness~\cite{altamimi2026rspeak}; a wearable system lets users choose direct output or expansion~\cite{tang2026throat}.
AAC generation therefore already has controls, optional expansion and fidelity evaluation.
We add a conditional specification of \emph{which} commitments are testable under each input, and a test of the oracle.
Accessibility engineering spans the life cycle~\cite{paiva2021accessibility}; conformance checks cannot see whether generated text keeps the user's meaning.

\section{Input-Conditioned Requirements}
\label{sec:contracts}
%% =====================================================================

\paragraph*{Families and channels}
A \emph{family} is a minimal pair of authored source messages that share content words and differ on one \emph{critical} dimension: polarity, agent (who acts), certainty, speech act (question, request or statement), politeness, or time/quantity.
Each variant is rendered through five channels, shown for one family in Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}B: \emph{bare} keywords; \emph{+word}, which adds one noncritical content word; \emph{+chip}, which adds the critical cue as a bracketed tag (\chip{NOT}); \emph{+all}, with every tag the variant needs; and \emph{full}, the source sentence itself.
Every variant needs at least one tag, so the unmarked reading gets a counter-tag (e.g., \chip{PLAIN}) and neither variant passes for free.
Tags are a test device for supplying a cue, not a proposed interface.

\paragraph*{Requirements}
For output $y$ from input $x$, let $L_d(y)$ be the extracted value on dimension $d$ and $C(x)$ the commitments that $x$ carries.
\textbf{R1} (supplied cue): if $C(x)$ fixes $d$ to $c$, $L_d(y)$ must be admissible for $c$.
\textbf{R2} (full input): if $x$ is the full source, each scored explicit value is kept, including the \emph{absence} of a politeness marker.
\textbf{R3} (ambiguity): if $C(x)$ does not fix $d$, $L_d(y)$ is not pass/fail; a committed value is logged as an \emph{Assumption}, and its direction is reported, because a systematic default decides which reading is free.
\textbf{R4} (composition): adding a compatible cue for $e$ must not break a satisfied value on $d\neq e$.
\textbf{R5} (input builder): a test input that claims to supply commitment $c$ must actually display a cue for $c$; otherwise failures cannot be attributed to the model (\S\ref{sec:wb}).

\paragraph*{Verdicts are separate checks}
\emph{Loss} records that the tested channel does not fix the value; \emph{Violation} that a supplied value was not kept (R1/R2); \emph{Assumption} that the output committed to a value the input left open (R3).
They are not exclusive: \say{I feel well.} from \kw{feel well} is both a Loss (the channel never carried the negation) and an Assumption (the output chose affirmative), and no Violation.
A reference-fidelity score would instead fail it against \say{I'm not feeling well today.}, charging the model for what the interface omitted; a similarity score would pass Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}A, although the input supplied the plain wording.
With balanced pairs, a bare output matches at most one variant, so 50\% paired match is a design ceiling under ambiguity, not a target.

\paragraph*{Executable checks}
\MR{2} (cue restoration) and \MR{3} (full preservation) are single-output contract checks of R1/R2 on cue and full channels.
\MR{4} relates a single-cue output to a two-cue output and counts pass$\to$fail transitions of the protected value (R4).
\MR{1} compares bare with +word; both leave $d$ open, so a changed label is a default-sensitivity diagnostic and a length control, not a defect.
\MR{0} tests the oracle: an unchanged source must pass.
Verdicts are computed in code from labels, the item specification and the channel, so a rule change re-scores all outputs without model calls.

\section{A Partial Oracle and Its Failure Envelope}
\label{sec:oracle}
%% =====================================================================
The oracle decides only whether a fixed set of explicit, text-level values is kept.
It does not judge fluency, appropriateness, referents or whether anyone would accept the sentence.

\paragraph*{Extraction as run}
An extractor (gpt-6.1-sol, a different model from the GPT model under test) answers the family's critical question and five general questions.
Critical and general extraction see the output only; added-content extraction also sees the visible input.
Two prompt variants (J1, J2) differ in wording and option order.
Critical disagreements are resolved per run.
On the discovery set, a cross-family arbiter (claude-opus-5-5) decides them, otherwise the label is unknown; the arbiter may see the visible input, which in full mode reveals the source.
On W-C, the arbiter resolved \RES{wc_arb_rows} rows (\RES{wc_arb_units} units) after outcomes were known, from an output-only view; the pre-arbitration analysis, which masks them as unknown, is reported beside it (\S\ref{sec:wc}).
W-D uses a J1 fallback on \RES{wd_j1_rows} of \RES{wd_rows} scored rows.
General-dimension ties are broken by a rule-based extractor that the guard in \S\ref{sec:decisions} also uses, which limits their independence.

\paragraph*{Testing the oracle}
Table~\ref{tab:oracle} lists the evidence.
Each authored test source was injected as its own full-channel output (identity) and with one hand-written single-value mutation, frozen before judging.
A second, hand-written challenge set has four groups of six controls and six one-feature mutants, keys hashed before the first judge call; its pre-specified gate (\(\geq\)22/24 mutants detected, \(\leq\)1/24 false alarms) passes when pooled, with no slack.

\paragraph*{A defect, a repair, and its blind spot}
Before these injections, \MR{0} on stored model outputs found that the original oracle failed \RES{id_fail_raw} of \RES{id_n} reproductions of the source, all one sentence, \say{Yes, you can borrow my car this weekend.}, coded \emph{statement} against the target \emph{acceptance} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}E).
The model had done its job; the checker demanded a distinction the text need not force.
We relaxed the rule so that \emph{statement} satisfies an acceptance target.
The keyed challenges then showed the cost: the relaxed oracle detects \kof{or_acc_mut_det} acceptance mutants and misses both statement substitutions (\say{You borrowed my car this weekend.}), whereas without the relaxation it detects \kof{or_new_mut_det_raw}.
Removing a false alarm removed fault sensitivity.
We therefore quarantine both refusal/acceptance families from every primary result (leaving \RES{n_fam_primary} of \RES{n_fam_test} discovery families) until a predicate passes both identity and mutation tests.

\paragraph*{Human gate}
Two annotators coded \RES{human_n_items} items with the extractor's codebook and its output-only view: a \RES{human_multicoded}-item core coded by both (\RES{human_multicodedsampled} sampled outputs, \RES{human_multicodedchallenge} challenge sentences) and \RES{human_singlecoded} further outputs coded by one.
Labels were converted from another format; the files carry no per-item timing.
The reference is the plurality label; core disagreements (at most \RES{human_noconsensus_max} per field) are excluded, not adjudicated.
The pre-specified gate ($\kappa\geq0.6$ on held-out items) passes on all \RES{human_gate_n} fields (Table~\ref{tab:human}); the lowest lower bound is \RES{human_gkappa_lo_min}.

\paragraph*{Humans caught a shared machine error}
On \say{I can't wait to go to the party.} both judge prompts, the rule-based extractor and two further machine coders answered \emph{negated}; both annotators answered \emph{affirmative}, as keyed.
The machines agreed perfectly there and were wrong together, so machine agreement is not validation.
Humans did not resolve every departure: both read \say{a couple of painkillers} as \emph{two}, as the machines did, against our frozen key \emph{unclear}; we keep the key and report the disagreement.
On critical labels against the keys, the annotators score \kn{human_chkey_A1} and \kn{human_chkey_A2}, the judge \kn{human_chkey_judge}.
The gate validates extraction of explicit markers on the discovery inventory; it does not validate repaired text, W-B readings or every new W-C/W-D boundary case.

\section{Evidence Populations}
\label{sec:setup}
%% =====================================================================
All items are authored or crowd-written, not messages from AAC users.
\emph{Discovery}: \RES{n_fam_test} families, \RES{n_fam_dim} per dimension, of which \RES{n_fam_primary} are primary after quarantine; prompts and codebook were developed on separate pilot and development families.
\emph{W-C} (confirmatory): \RES{wc_n_fam_incl} new families (80 each for polarity, speech act and politeness), frozen and pre-registered with three contrasts; \RES{wc_n_fam} are analyzed after a pre-generation exclusion of two speech-act families with a codebook conflict.
\emph{W-D} (composition): \RES{wd_n_scen} physical scenarios crossing \chip{YES}/\chip{NOT} with \chip{I}/\chip{YOU} under two encodings of the constant statement cue, two greedy draws each.
\emph{W-B} (transfer): 160 crowd-written fictional AAC messages from AACText~\cite{vertanen2011imagination}, 80 random and 80 enriched for rare cues, never pooled; \RES{wb_msgs_restored} have a restored-cue channel.
Three open models run locally with 4-bit quantization (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-3-4B-it) and one API model (gpt-6.1-terra).
P0 asks for one sentence (greedy); P2 asks for three suggestions with different meanings; S3 draws three P0 samples at temperature 0.7.
Discovery intervals use 2,000 whole-family resamples, W-C 10,000 resamples of recorded seed/construction clusters, W-D 10,000 resamples of physical scenarios, W-B message resamples; all are pointwise 95\% percentile intervals over a fixed model roster, and zero observed events do not establish zero risk.

Speech act did not reach full-input fidelity: one tag kept the act in \pct{wcm_act_chip1} of targets against \pct{wcm_act_full} with full input, a paired gap of \RES{wc_act_chip1_full}\,pp \ci{wc_act_chip1_full}.
Question tags failed in \kn{wc_qfail} targets (\pct{wc_qfail} \cip{wc_qfail}); \RES{wc_q2req_k} of these became requests, as when \kw{[?] parcel signature}, from a question about whether a signature is needed, became \say{Can you sign for this parcel?} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}C).
Focal checks overstate preservation when several cues are present: with all tags, joint preservation of every supplied predicate was \pct{wcj_act_chipall} for speech act (focal \pct{wcm_act_chipall}) and \pct{wcj_pz_chipall} for politeness (focal \pct{wcm_pz_chipall}).
Asking for three different meanings (P2) produced both polarity readings in only \kn{wccov_p2} bare sets, against \kn{wccov_s3} for three samples.
Diversity can also cut the wrong way: under full input, \kn{ap_xp_p2} P2 polarity sets contained the \emph{other} source sentence verbatim (\kn{ap_xp_s3} for S3; exploratory, judge-free string match).

\subsection{Composition is a per-model, per-encoding regression (R4)}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{r2d/fig_composition_col}
  \caption{W-D: protected-value pass$\to$fail transitions when a second cue is added, minus disagreement when the identical single-cue input is regenerated (640 transitions and 160 repeat pairs per point; 95\% CIs over 80 scenarios). Two encodings of the same statement cue.}
  \label{fig:wd}
\end{figure}
In W-D (Fig.~\ref{fig:wd}), adding \chip{YOU} to \kw{[NOT] [STATEMENT] lock door} turned Llama's \say{I will not lock the door.} into \say{You lock the door.}
Across scenarios, Llama lost an already-kept negation in \kn{wdll} transitions, in \RES{wdll_scen} of \RES{wd_n_scen} scenarios, against \kn{wdll_rep} disagreements when the identical single-cue input was regenerated: \RES{wdll_d}\,pp \ci{wdll_d} above that floor, and \RES{wdll_db}\,pp \ci{wdll_db} above the floor for two-cue inputs (\kn{wdll_brep}).
All \RES{wd_notyou_k} damaged transitions were in the NOT$\times$YOU cell (\kn{wd_notyou}, against \kn{wd_other} in the other three cells; exploratory decomposition), and all lack the checked negation markers.
The other three models showed \RES{wd_stmt_pol_other_k} such transitions under this encoding.
Under the alternative \chip{TELL} encoding Llama kept negation, but Qwen lost the agent in \kn{wdqa} transitions (\RES{wdqa_scen} scenarios), and in \RES{wdqa_stmt_k} under \chip{STATEMENT}; some of those outputs literalize the tag, and their agent labels depend on a codebook rule that the judges did not apply consistently.
Neither encoding is safe for every model, and constraint order is an untested alternative explanation~\cite{zeng2025order}.
The engineering consequence is narrow: composition checks must be run per model and per encoding, with a repeat-draw floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:wd}).

\subsection{Corpus transfer and an input-contract defect (W-B)}
\label{sec:wb}
On AACText messages compressed to four content words, on dimensions the panel judged ambiguous, outputs took the unmarked reading in \pct{wbr_def} \cip{wbr_def} of cases (random sample) and \pct{wbe_def} (enriched).
A strictly negated source was kept in \kn{wbr_neg} random and \kn{wbe_neg} enriched outputs.
Restoring the focal cue raised source match by \RES{wbr_gain} and \RES{wbe_gain}\,pp.
Auditing all \RES{wb_msgs_restored} restorations against R5 exposed a defect in our own input builder: \RES{wb_conflicts} displayed a chip that contradicts the stored source category, e.g., \chip{MAYBE} for a plain assertion, after a fallback in the builder.
In the five \chip{MAYBE} cases, all \RES{wb_maybe_n} outputs followed the visible chip and failed the source, which a source-fidelity score counts as model failures.
Scored against what the input actually displayed, general-predicate fidelity was \pct{wbr_actual} \cip{wbr_actual} (random) and \pct{wbe_actual} \cip{wbe_actual} (enriched); \RES{wb_unknown} further restorations stay unknown for source attribution (unclear or disputed references, ambiguous words).
Full-source category mismatches (\pct{wbr_vfull} and \pct{wbe_vfull}) partly reflect differences between the source panel's and the judge's codebooks.
W-B readings are machine-assessed; its 20-message human audit has not been completed.

Table~\ref{tab:decisions} shows how each requirement becomes a check, what the check found, what a developer could decide, and what the evidence does not cover.
The full-mode row is the simplest lesson: if the person can say \say{this is my sentence}, the requirement is met by not generating at all; whether full mode is offered and discoverable is a product decision.
The guard row shows that the same checks can run at run time; its benefits, residual failures and displaced choices are evaluated separately in companion work and are not claimed here.
The oracle and input-builder rows show why test infrastructure needs its own tests: our first oracle repair silently weakened the oracle, and our input builder silently supplied the wrong cue.

\paragraph*{Recommendations}
Write each requirement conditional on the input and per dimension: \say{preserve meaning} is not testable, \say{if the input carries \chip{NOT}, the output is negated} is.
Test with minimal pairs, keep defaults separate from failures, and report joint as well as focal preservation.
Treat added politeness on typed text as a failing test and make style assistance opt-in.
Give the oracle identity and keyed-mutation tests and re-run them after every relaxation; validate generated test inputs against their specification.
Gate composition per model and encoding, against repeat draws.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
%% =====================================================================
\emph{Construct.} Labels are coarse, explicit categories; \say{plain} means no explicit marker, and content, referents, negation scope and certainty degree are only partly covered.
A passing check is not a correct sentence (Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}D).
\emph{Oracle.} All rates depend on an LLM extractor; the human gate covers explicit markers on the discovery inventory with two annotators, plurality references and excluded ties, not subtler readings, W-B readings, repaired text, or every W-C/W-D boundary.
The planned 20-item W-C human source check was not completed (\RES{n_returns_wc} returns).
\emph{Chronology.} W-C has early cached requests and post-hoc arbitration; W-D's item and codebook freeze is not independently established and uses a J1 fallback.
\emph{Selection and scale.} Four fixed models; examples were selected after outcomes by declared rules; discovery has ten families per dimension, W-C covers three dimensions.
\emph{Heterogeneity.} The politeness and composition effects are model- and encoding-specific.
\emph{Cost.} Selection counts omit reading, navigation and fatigue~\cite{frisch2026complicated}.
\emph{No users.} We cannot speak to benefit, agency or whether anyone wants a given output, and we assume no particular language style of any group of AAC users~\cite{zisk2019aac,donaldson2021everyone}.

\section{Conclusion}
%% =====================================================================
Even a sentence the person typed in full can come back changed.
Conditioning requirements on what the input carried separates that violation from the guesses an interface forces when it collects less, and turns both into executable checks with a developer decision attached.
On fresh items, the registered contrasts held, with smaller and model-specific effects than in discovery.
Testing the test infrastructure mattered as much as testing the models.
Families, requirements, harness, oracle tests, stored outputs and analysis code are available at \url{https://github.com/1unarzDev/aac_voice_preservation}.
