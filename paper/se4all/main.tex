\begin{abstract}
In a constructed test, an LLM sentence expander for augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), Gemma-3-4B, was given a sentence typed in full, \say{Open this pickle jar for me.}, and returned \say{Please open this pickle jar for me.}
Whether such an edit is a defect depends on what the input specified: here the whole sentence was supplied, so the expander changed a choice it was given.
When a person selects only keywords, by contrast, words such as \say{not} or \say{please} may never reach the model, and its guess is a default, not a violation.
We turn this distinction into executable requirements that score a choice only when the input carried it, and test them with an oracle, an automated checker of a few explicit features, whose own failure modes we also test.
On \RES{wc_n_fam} analyzed fresh families (\RES{wc_n_fam_incl} designed) and four models, all three pre-registered contrasts held after Holm correction.
For instance, on \RES{wc_nfam_pz} politeness families, fully typed plain requests gained a politeness marker in \kn{wcpzadd} outputs and polite ones lost it in \kn{wcpzrem}, with most additions from one model.
The checker's extraction agreed with two human annotators on held-out items at $\kappa=\RES{human_gkappa_min}$--$\RES{human_gkappa_max}$ across seven label fields.
Testing the checker mattered: relaxing a rule to remove false alarms also hid real substitutions, so the affected families are quarantined.
\end{abstract}

LLM keyword expansion turns such a selection into a sentence~\cite{shen2022kwickchat,cai2024speakfaster}; some systems also let the person send a sentence directly~\cite{tang2026throat}.

Whether an expansion is wrong depends on what the input carried.
The keywords \kw{feel well} do not say whether the person feels well; Qwen2.5-7B wrote \say{I feel well.} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}, card~1).
No model can be blamed for that guess, but the interface decides who must pay an extra selection to say \say{not}.
Given \kw{Keywords: [NOT] feel well}, the same model wrote \say{I don't feel well.}

The input can also carry everything.
In a constructed full-input test, Gemma-3-4B was given the typed sentence \say{Open this pickle jar for me.} and added \say{Please} (card~2).
On the \RES{wc_nfam_pz} fresh politeness families of our confirmatory set and four models, plain typed sentences gained a politeness marker in \kn{wcpzadd} outputs (\pct{wcpzadd}), and polite ones lost theirs in \kn{wcpzrem} (Fig.~\ref{fig:ratchet}).
The pattern is concentrated, not universal: Gemma added a marker in \kn{wcpzadd_gemma} and gpt-6.1-terra in \kn{wcpzadd_terra}, whereas Llama and Qwen removed markers more often than they added them.
A developer needs to know which case a failed reference match is: a choice the input never carried calls for a better input channel; a choice the model received and changed calls for a preservation check; a fully typed sentence may need no generation at all.

Our policy for a declared full-input mode is a software requirement, not a claim about anyone's private intent: do not change typed wording unless the person asks for rewriting.
Exact copy satisfies that policy in full.
Our experiments test a weaker, partial version of it: whether selected explicit features, such as a politeness marker or its absence, polarity and speech act, survive.
A sentence without \say{please} may or may not reflect a deliberate choice, but adding one changes how a message reads: politeness suggestions shift how writers phrase messages and how they perceive their social relationship~\cite{zhang2026politeness}.
We test these features with an \emph{oracle} in the software-testing sense: an automated checker of a few explicit features, not an authority on meaning.

We contribute
(1)~\emph{input-conditioned requirements} (\S\ref{sec:contracts}) that score a choice only when the visible input supplied it, run over controlled input channels of minimal-pair families;
(2)~\emph{evidence} (\S\ref{sec:results}): pre-registered contrasts on fresh items, a model- and encoding-specific regression, and a corpus transfer that exposed a defect in our own input builder;
(3)~\emph{a partial oracle that is itself tested} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}), including a defect that our own repair introduced, with a passed human-agreement gate; and
(4)~\emph{developer decisions} attached to each requirement (\S\ref{sec:decisions}), including a copy baseline for typed sentences.
We ran no study with AAC users and make no claim about benefit, effort or preference.

\section{Input-Conditioned Requirements}
\label{sec:contracts}
%% =====================================================================

\paragraph*{Families and channels}
A \emph{family} is a minimal pair of authored source messages that share content words and differ on one \emph{critical} dimension: polarity, agent (who acts), certainty, speech act (question, request or statement), politeness, or time/quantity.
Each variant is rendered through five channels, four of which appear in Fig.~\ref{fig:gallery}, card~1: \emph{bare} keywords; \emph{+word}, which adds one noncritical content word; \emph{+chip}, which adds the critical cue as a bracketed tag (\chip{NOT}); \emph{+all}, with every tag the variant needs; and \emph{full}, the source sentence itself.
Every variant needs at least one tag, so the unmarked reading gets a counter-tag (e.g., \chip{PLAIN}) and neither variant passes for free.
Tags are a test device for supplying a cue, not a proposed interface.

\paragraph*{Requirements}
For output $y$ from input $x$, let $L_d(y)$ be the extracted value on dimension $d$ and $C(x)$ the commitments that $x$ carries.
\textbf{R1} (supplied cue): if $C(x)$ fixes $d$ to $c$, $L_d(y)$ must be admissible for $c$.
\textbf{R2} (full input): if $x$ is the full source, each scored explicit value is kept, including the \emph{absence} of a politeness marker.
\textbf{R3} (ambiguity): if $C(x)$ does not fix $d$, $L_d(y)$ is not pass/fail; a committed value is logged as an \emph{Assumption}, and its direction is reported, because a systematic default decides which reading is free.
\textbf{R4} (composition): adding a compatible cue for $e$ must not break a satisfied value on $d\neq e$.
\textbf{R5} (input builder): a test input that claims to supply commitment $c$ must actually display a cue for $c$; otherwise failures cannot be attributed to the model (\S\ref{sec:wb}).

\paragraph*{Verdicts}
A \emph{Violation} means a supplied value was not kept (R1/R2); an \emph{Assumption} means the output committed to a value the input left open (R3).
In card~1, \say{I feel well.} from \kw{feel well} is an Assumption and no Violation.
A reference-fidelity score would instead fail it against \say{I'm not feeling well today.}, charging the model for what the interface omitted; a similarity score would pass card~2, although the input supplied the plain wording.
With balanced pairs, a bare output matches at most one variant, so 50\% paired match is a design ceiling under ambiguity, not a target.

\paragraph*{Executable checks}
A \emph{cue check} (R1) and a \emph{full-input check} (R2) test single outputs on their channels.
A \emph{composition check} (R4) compares a one-cue output with the two-cue output for the same item and counts pass$\to$fail transitions of the protected value.
An \emph{identity check} tests the oracle itself: an unchanged source must pass.
Comparing bare with +word is only a default-sensitivity and length diagnostic, since both leave $d$ open.

\section{Evidence Populations and the Checker}
\label{sec:setup}
%% =====================================================================
All items are authored or crowd-written, not messages from AAC users.
\emph{Discovery}: \RES{n_fam_test} families, \RES{n_fam_dim} per dimension, of which \RES{n_fam_primary} are primary after a quarantine (\S\ref{sec:oracle}); prompts and codebook were developed on separate pilot and development families.
\emph{W-C} (confirmatory): \RES{wc_n_fam_incl} new families, frozen and pre-registered with three contrasts; \RES{wc_n_fam} are analyzed (\RES{wc_nfam_pol} polarity, \RES{wc_nfam_act} speech act, \RES{wc_nfam_pz} politeness) after a pre-generation exclusion of two speech-act families with a codebook conflict.
\emph{W-D} (composition): \RES{wd_n_scen} physical scenarios crossing \chip{YES}/\chip{NOT} with \chip{I}/\chip{YOU} under two encodings of the constant statement cue, two greedy draws each.
\emph{W-B} (transfer): 160 crowd-written fictional AAC messages from AACText~\cite{vertanen2011imagination}, 80 random and 80 enriched for rare cues, never pooled; \RES{wb_msgs_restored} have a restored-cue channel.
Three open models run locally with 4-bit quantization (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-3-4B-it) and one API model (gpt-6.1-terra).
Prompt P0 asks for one sentence (greedy); P2 asks for three suggestions with different meanings; S3 draws three P0 samples at temperature 0.7.
Discovery intervals use 2,000 whole-family resamples, W-C 10,000 resamples of recorded seed/construction clusters, W-D 10,000 resamples of physical scenarios, W-B message resamples; all are pointwise 95\% percentile intervals over a fixed model roster, and zero observed events do not establish zero risk.

\paragraph*{The checker as run}
The oracle decides only whether a fixed set of explicit, text-level values is kept; it does not judge fluency, appropriateness, referents or whether anyone would accept the sentence.
An extractor (gpt-6.1-sol, a different model from the GPT model under test) answers the family's critical question and five general questions from the output alone; added-content extraction also sees the visible input.
Verdicts are then computed in code from labels, item specification and channel, so a rule change re-scores every output without model calls.
Two extractor prompts (J1, J2) differ in wording and option order; critical disagreements go to a cross-family arbiter (claude-opus-5-5) or stay unknown.
On discovery the arbiter may see the visible input, which in full mode reveals the source; on W-C it resolved \RES{wc_arb_rows} rows (\RES{wc_arb_units} units) after outcomes were known, from an output-only view, and the pre-arbitration analysis is reported beside it; W-D uses a J1 fallback on \RES{wd_j1_rows} of \RES{wd_rows} scored rows.
General-dimension ties are broken by a rule-based extractor that the guard in \S\ref{sec:decisions} also uses, which limits their independence.

\subsection{Corpus transfer and an input-contract defect (W-B)}
\label{sec:wb}
On AACText messages compressed to four content words, on dimensions the panel judged ambiguous, outputs took the unmarked reading in \pct{wbr_def} \cip{wbr_def} of cases (random sample) and \pct{wbe_def} (enriched).
A strictly negated source was kept in \kn{wbr_neg} random and \kn{wbe_neg} enriched outputs.
Restoring the focal cue raised source match by \RES{wbr_gain} and \RES{wbe_gain}\,pp.
Auditing all \RES{wb_msgs_restored} restorations against R5 exposed a defect in our own input builder: \RES{wb_conflicts} displayed a chip that contradicts the stored source category, e.g., \chip{MAYBE} for a plain assertion, after a fallback in the builder.
In the five \chip{MAYBE} cases, all \RES{wb_maybe_n} outputs followed the visible chip and failed the source, which a source-fidelity score counts as model failures.
A validator that decodes only the rendered input now rejects all \RES{r5_conf} before generation; on all \RES{wb_msgs_restored} it reproduces the audit and never certifies the \RES{r5_sure} coarser \chip{SURE} restorations (Table~\ref{tab:decisions}).
Scored against what the input actually displayed, general-predicate fidelity was \pct{wbr_actual} \cip{wbr_actual} (random) and \pct{wbe_actual} \cip{wbe_actual} (enriched); \RES{wb_unknown} further restorations stay unknown for source attribution (unclear or disputed references, ambiguous words).
These are separate endpoints on different populations, not an improvement.
Full-source category mismatches (\pct{wbr_vfull} and \pct{wbe_vfull}) partly reflect differences between the source panel's and the judge's codebooks.
W-B readings are machine-assessed; its 20-message human audit has not been completed.

\paragraph*{A defect, a repair, and its blind spot}
The identity check on stored model outputs found that the original oracle failed \RES{id_fail_raw} of \RES{id_n} reproductions of the source, all one sentence, \say{Yes, you can borrow my car this weekend.}, coded \emph{statement} against the target \emph{acceptance} (Fig.~\ref{fig:checker}).
The model had done its job; the checker demanded a distinction the text need not force.
We relaxed the rule so that \emph{statement} satisfies an acceptance target.
Keyed challenges then showed the cost: the relaxed oracle detects \kof{or_acc_mut_det} acceptance mutants and misses both statement substitutions (\say{You borrowed my car this weekend.}), whereas without the relaxation it detects \kof{or_new_mut_det_raw} mutants across groups.
Removing a false alarm removed fault sensitivity.
We therefore quarantine both refusal/acceptance families from every primary result (leaving \RES{n_fam_primary} of \RES{n_fam_test} discovery families) until a predicate passes both identity and mutation tests.

\paragraph*{Identity and mutation tests}
Table~\ref{tab:oracle} lists the evidence.
Each authored test source was injected as its own full-channel output (identity) and with one hand-written single-value mutation, frozen before judging.
A second, hand-written challenge set has four groups of six controls and six one-feature mutants, keys hashed before the first judge call; its pre-specified gate (\(\geq\)22/24 mutants detected, \(\leq\)1/24 false alarms) passes when pooled, with no slack.

\paragraph*{Humans caught a shared machine error}
On \say{I can't wait to go to the party.} both judge prompts, the rule-based extractor and two further machine coders answered \emph{negated}; both annotators answered \emph{affirmative}, as keyed.
The machines agreed perfectly there and were wrong together, so machine agreement is not validation.
Humans did not resolve every departure: both read \say{a couple of painkillers} as \emph{two}, as the machines did, against our frozen key \emph{unclear}; we keep the key and report the disagreement.
On critical labels against the keys, the annotators score \kn{human_chkey_A1} and \kn{human_chkey_A2}, the judge \kn{human_chkey_judge}.
The gate validates extraction of explicit markers on the discovery inventory; it does not validate repaired text, W-B readings or every new W-C/W-D boundary case.

Table~\ref{tab:decisions} shows how each requirement becomes a check, what the check found, what a developer could decide, and what the evidence does not cover.
The full-mode row is the simplest lesson: if the person can say \say{this is my sentence}, the requirement is met by not generating at all; whether full mode is offered and discoverable is a product decision.
The guard row shows that the same checks can run at run time.
On discovery cue channels, regeneration passes more targets than zero-call marker stripping, \RES{c3_guard_increment}\,pp \ci{c3_guard_increment} (\RES{c3_guard_increment_clusters} family clusters; exploratory, paired stored configurations), but this does not establish that the feedback causes the gain.
Frozen and rerun on W-C's \RES{gtw_fam} speech-act families (\RES{gtw_targets} targets), the guard passed its pre-fixed transfer criteria, raising scored joint pass by \RES{gtw_jgain}\,pp \ci{gtw_jgain} while changing \pct{gtw_cc} (\kn{gtw_cc}) of correct texts with \kn{gtw_pf} pass$\to$fail, on the same partial predicates; companion work analyzes it.

The oracle and input-builder rows show why test infrastructure needs its own tests: our first oracle repair silently weakened the oracle, and our input builder silently supplied the wrong cue.

\paragraph*{Recommendations}
Write each requirement conditional on the input and per dimension: \say{preserve meaning} is not testable, \say{if the input carries \chip{NOT}, the output is negated} is.
Test with minimal pairs, keep defaults separate from failures, and report joint as well as focal preservation.
Treat added politeness on typed text as a failing test and make style assistance opt-in.
Give the oracle identity and keyed-mutation tests and re-run them after every relaxation; validate generated test inputs against their specification before generation, as our validator does.
Gate composition per model and encoding, against repeat draws.

\section{Related Work}
%% =====================================================================
\paragraph*{Testing LLM systems}
CheckList organizes behavioral tests into minimum-functionality, invariance and directional tests~\cite{ribeiro2020checklist}; Cho et al.\ catalogue metamorphic relations for LLM-based NLP~\cite{cho2025metamorphic}; COLLIE tests compositional generation constraints~\cite{yao2024collie}.
Sensei combines a user simulator with a DSL for assertions and metamorphic relations~\cite{delara2026sensei}.
Extracting fields, asserting properties and relating test cases are therefore established; we claim no new testing technique.
LLM judges show position, verbosity and self-preference biases~\cite{zheng2023judging,panickssery2024selfpreference}, similarity metrics can miss negation~\cite{koch2022selfeval}, and LLM politeness judgments agree with each other more than with humans~\cite{wang2026politemisaligned}.
Pauli et al.\ show that content-preservation metrics look valid when test data confound the intended style change with content errors, and build challenge data that vary them separately~\cite{pauli2025stylegap}.
Our identity and keyed-mutation tests apply that idea to a checker: an authorized change must pass and an unauthorized one must fail.

\paragraph*{Preservation during rewriting}
Qu et al.\ condition style transfer on the source dialog act and evaluate act retention~\cite{qu2023conditioning}; preserving a speech act while rewriting is thus not new.
LLMs favor particular politeness strategies in free production~\cite{zhao2025politeness}, and rewriting can keep semantic similarity while erasing pragmatic markers, which preservation prompts reduce but do not remove~\cite{navneet2026voice}.
Wang et al.\ report that LLM detoxification rewrites often default to overly polite wording that distorts communicative intent, and evaluate content and sentiment preservation jointly~\cite{wang2025toxirewrite}; neither that concern nor joint fidelity evaluation is ours.
Their sentiment polarity is emotional stance, not grammatical negation, and their task requests an edit.
Our focus is narrower: whether a marker the person supplied, or explicitly did not supply, survives when no edit was requested.

\paragraph*{LLMs in AAC}
Systems studied with AAC users~\cite{valencia2023less} expose intent controls such as intent buttons~\cite{valencia2024compa}, intention choices for people with aphasia~\cite{mao2025designprobes} and stance tags~\cite{xiang2025socializechat}.
Lacosse et al.\ keep bracketed function words such as \say{not}~\cite{lacosse2026cognitivesteering}; Choudhury et al.\ test whether affect cues reach the output~\cite{choudhury2025alignment}; Gorenshtein et al.\ benchmark intent drift under decoder errors~\cite{gorenshtein2026intentdrift}; R-SPEAK was co-designed with people with aphasia and scores faithfulness~\cite{altamimi2026rspeak}; a wearable system lets users choose direct output or expansion~\cite{tang2026throat}.
AAC generation therefore already has controls, optional expansion and fidelity evaluation.
We add a conditional specification of \emph{which} commitments are testable under each input, and a test of the oracle.
Delegation is not always unwanted: in one study, AAC users were willing to trade agency for efficiency when the timing of humorous comments mattered~\cite{weinberg2025humor}.
Our requirements therefore attach to the mode the person chose, literal realization rather than ideation.
Accessibility engineering spans the life cycle~\cite{paiva2021accessibility}; conformance checks cannot see whether generated text keeps the user's meaning.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
%% =====================================================================
\emph{Construct.} Labels are coarse, explicit categories; \say{plain} means no explicit marker, and content, referents, negation scope and certainty degree are only partly covered.
Passing the checker is weaker than keeping the typed wording, and a passing check is not a correct sentence (Fig.~\ref{fig:guard}).
\emph{Oracle.} All rates depend on an LLM extractor; the human gate covers explicit markers on the discovery inventory with two annotators, plurality references and excluded ties, not subtler readings, W-B readings, repaired text, or every W-C/W-D boundary.
The planned 20-item W-C human source check was not completed (\RES{n_returns_wc} returns).
\emph{Chronology.} W-C has early cached requests and post-hoc arbitration; W-D's item and codebook freeze is not independently established and uses a J1 fallback.
\emph{Selection and scale.} Four fixed models; examples were selected after outcomes by declared rules; discovery has ten families per dimension, W-C covers three dimensions.
\emph{Heterogeneity.} The politeness and composition effects are model- and encoding-specific.
\emph{Cost.} Selection counts omit reading, navigation and fatigue~\cite{frisch2026complicated}.
\emph{No users.} All examples are constructed tests; we cannot speak to benefit, agency or whether anyone wants a given output, and we assume no particular language style of any group of AAC users~\cite{zisk2019aac,donaldson2021everyone}.
No autistic advisers took part; future work should involve autistic AAC users and advisers directly.

\section{Conclusion}
%% =====================================================================
Even a sentence supplied in full can come back changed.
Conditioning requirements on what the input carried separates that violation from the guesses an interface forces when it collects less, and turns both into executable checks with a developer decision attached.
On fresh items, the registered contrasts held, with smaller and model-specific effects than in discovery.
Testing the test infrastructure mattered as much as testing the models.
Families, requirements, harness, oracle tests, stored outputs, example manifests and analysis code are available at \url{https://github.com/1unarzDev/aac_voice_preservation}.

\section*{Ethics and Positionality}
No data were collected from AAC users; AACText is public under CC BY 4.0.
Two anonymous annotators, neither an author, labeled model outputs under a fixed codebook, blinded as designed: they saw only each output sentence, plus the visible input when coding added content, with no model, condition or source shown; only an annotator ID was recorded.
As independent researchers we did not obtain an institutional review determination; annotators labelled machine-generated sentences, were reimbursed, and no personal data were collected. % AUTHOR-CONFIRM: ethics sentence
No autistic advisers or community reviewers took part.
Instead, we addressed the problems of an earlier version of this study through expert literature and literature reviews: a critical review of that version retired its synthetic autistic-vs-neurotypical comparison; the design draws on autism and participatory-research guidance, including AASPIRE~\cite{nicolaidis2019aaspire} and the double-empathy~\cite{milton2012double} and heterogeneity~\cite{zisk2019aac} cautions; we assume no \say{autistic style}; and our claims are limited to commitments explicit in the text.
