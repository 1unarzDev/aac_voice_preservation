# TACCESS associate-editor view: ten actions under "no IRB, no participant studies"

Role: associate editor, ACM TACCESS (journal acceptance). Read: SE4All and CALE-AI text (pdftotext), journal draft (35 pp, ~23.5k words, 5 section files + appendix) and OUTLINE.md, ledger rows C54-C81, journal limitations section. I recomputed nothing. Items marked [verify] rest on outside knowledge (ACM policy, corpora).

## Where the journal paper stands (my rating today)
- Recommendation if submitted now: major revision, leaning reject-and-resubmit at TACCESS. Scale 1-5 (5 = accept): 2.5.
- Why: TACCESS reviewers weigh "evidence about, with, and for disabled people". The paper has strong machine-side and materials-side measurement (cue restoration +37.5 pp, 10 writers; seeded-fault sweep; independent mutants; listener study) but zero AAC-user outcome data. Second, it is 35 pages with 5 stacked contributions (harness, default pricing, guard, whole-message, ask-policy null); a reviewer cannot say what the one claim is. Third, several headline statements are weak in a way reviewers will quote: oracle catches 58.5% of lexicon-blind mutants; listener chip success is 28.3%; reversal difference interval includes 0; consultants/SLPs n=2.
- Realistic ceiling without IRB: 3.5-4 (minor/major revision, accepted on revision) IF framed as a participant-free technical evaluation and measurement-validity paper. A 4.5 needs AAC-user data; do not chase it.

### 3. Reposition the literature and the participatory stance (AAC-domain scholarship). Gain +0.4, feasibility high. (c); partial (a).
- Do: add 1.5 pages of AAC-field grounding: AAC as authorship/agency (Valencia 2020, Hancock 2020 already in), message-vs-access distinction, "nothing about us without us" and participatory-design norms in accessibility research, the "disabled-authored data" critique of using non-disabled proxies, and a table "what a non-AAC proxy can and cannot show". Verify every added citation in `lit/`; mark uncertain with `% VERIFY`. Also run and document a bounded systematic search (query strings, databases, date, inclusion rule; PRISMA-lite one paragraph) to support "not found among inspected studies" and remove any "first" claim.
- Why: the typical TACCESS reject comment is "authors do not engage with the AAC literature or community". This is the cheapest answer. A fair search protocol also protects novelty claims (SYNTHESIS already warns "no 'first' claim is safe").
- Effort: 1-2 days reading/writing, no compute. Risks: citing unseen papers. NOT: no performative community language ("co-designed", "community-driven") - the consultants are two paid design consultants, not partners; use that exact wording.

### 4. Frontier-model replication of the headline machine results (cue restoration, bare default, guard). Gain +0.35, feasibility medium-high. (b) for CALE-AI/SE4All camera-ready, (c) journal.
- Do: rerun the hw1+hw2 speech-act, polarity and politeness bare/noncrit/chip/word channels on 2-3 current hosted models (GPT-family latest tier already used, limited Gemini, one Claude model via subagents) with the same prompts, T=0 where possible, judged by the existing J1/J2 + the Claude relabel path (C77, 94.0%/kappa .935). Pre-declare in a PLAN.md: success = bare affirmative >= 90% and chip-minus-word within the existing 6-model range; any model that falls outside is reported, not dropped. Add as one row set in the six-model table.
- Why: the limitation "open models at most 14B, 4-bit" (SE4All threats; CALE-AI limitations) is the most quotable external-validity gap that CAN be closed without people. Reviewers ask "do frontier models still default affirmative?". Either answer is publishable: if the default shrinks, the paper becomes "default is model-dependent; here is the price", which is stronger than a universal-default claim.
- Effort: ~1 day, API cost small (hw1+hw2 ~6.6k requests/model x channels; use batch), Claude judge 1-2 h subagents. Risks: hosted API non-determinism (state it); result may weaken the headline (accept; honesty). NOT: do not tune prompts after seeing frontier results; do not mix in new items; do not call it "all LLMs".

### 5. Replace the post-hoc LLM/diff reason classifier with human reason coding (annotators + SLPs). Gain +0.3, feasibility high. (b)/(c).
- Do: Part 30. Take the 199 non-accepted Part 18 outputs (and the 130 judge-correct ones as the key stratum); 20 classmate annotators, ~10 items each (2 coders per item, ~20 min each, 400 codings), coding materials only: "compared with the writer's own sentence, the draft (a) adds something the writer did not say, (b) drops detail, (c) changes who does what, (d) changes critical value, (e) fluency/other". Freeze codebook and decision rule before opening returns; the classifier becomes a secondary column. Ask the 2 SLPs to double-code a 30-item subset (30 minutes each) for a clinical reading of "added vs dropped".
- Why: the "no output added content; loss is compression and event/role misresolution" claim is the paper's pivot and currently rests on a classifier written after seeing items and checked by an LLM hand coder (the journal limitations calls the kappa optimistic). Human reason coding turns the key claim from post hoc to checked.
- Effort: ~3 days elapsed (build packet 3 h, annotators 20 x 20 min = ~7 person-hours, scoring 3 h), no compute. Risks: humans disagree more than the classifier (report kappa, take human consensus as primary). NOT: do not ask writers again about their own intent (behavioural/self-report); only material-coding of existing pairs; no timing or reading-speed measurements.

### 6. Oracle independence: second-family and ensemble judge on the independent mutants (Part 29). Gain +0.3 (more at SE4All), feasibility high. (a) is possible for SE4All; (b) otherwise.
- Do: the 75 confirmed independent mutants and 46 controls already have human labels (95.3% agreement, kappa .91). Run them through (i) the Claude-subagent judge (as in C77), (ii) a pre-declared union and majority ensemble of GPT J1/J2 + Claude, no tuning on these items. Report lexicon-blind recall for single judge vs ensemble and the false-alarm count out of 46. Also report the 22 mutants the judge missed by class (role_passive 0/14, q_embed 1/12 were unusable; report them as a boundary of what the oracle can see).
- Why: 58.5% lexicon-blind recall is the number an SE reviewer will quote as "your oracle misses 4 in 10 meaning changes you did not anticipate". A declared ensemble result is either a fix or an honestly bounded limitation; both raise the score above silence. It uses existing human labels, so zero IRB exposure.
- Effort: 2-4 hours compute+subagents, 1 hour writing. Risks: ensemble raises FA on controls (report); post-hoc nature: declare the rule before the run and keep the original 70.7%/58.5% as the headline. NOT: do not retune the lexicon on these mutants and then re-report as independent; do not delete Part 29's original result.

## Dropped candidates (and why)
- New listener/draft-set study with timing or tap counts: timing is behavioural data about the annotators; keep taps as arithmetic with a stated assumption (rho = 1, F1 replaced the transport estimate).
- More SLPs: constraint is 2 anonymous raters; instead use them on high-value items (action 5 subset; severity of "unclear" outcomes in the listener study, 30 min each).
- More ask-policy or frontier pilots: not people-limited, but each adds length; the null is already reported (cut per action 1).

## Weaknesses that cannot be fixed without IRB, and framing that costs least
1. No AAC-user outcome (benefit, preference, burden, trust, agency). Frame: the paper is a participant-free technical evaluation and a requirements-and-measurement contribution; every human-effect statement is a prediction to be tested by others (CALE-AI already ends §7 as testable predictions). Use "listeners (adults, not AAC partners) comprehended the materials" never "people understood".
2. Keyword input is not generated the way AAC users generate it (no finished sentence, motor/time constraints, vocabulary layout). Frame as a scope: this is about the model's expansion given a keyword string; list the five things a user study must supply; action 9 gives the only partial check.
3. Real-world consequence of a reversal. Frame: severity is an expert-review weight (n=2 SLPs, not a population estimate); the reversal rate per 1,000 is a stored-text event rate, not harm. Keep "None of these indicators is a measured harm" (already in CALE-AI).
4. Requirements from n=2 consultants and n=2 SLPs. Frame: design consultation and expert review of materials; member-check (action 7); never "AAC-user requirements".
5. The q (cue supply) rate 73.6% and the reading cost of draft sets. Frame: q is measured by adult annotators choosing between written drafts (proxy); reading cost = words shown, a counted property of the draft set, not a time measured on people.
6. Ethics/IRB itself. Frame transparently and once: "no institutional review determination was sought; no participant was studied; classmates judged study materials, no data about them were collected; some wrote pairs under recorded CC BY consent." Risk I cannot remove: an AE or ACM may ask for a determination letter or treat classmate pair-writing and writer self-ratings (Part 18, where writers rate their own drafts) as human-subjects data [verify ACM policy on human-subject research]. Mitigation inside the constraint: describe Part 18 as annotation of materials built from the writer's own sentence, do not describe it as an evaluation of the writers, and put the ethics statement in the cover letter so it is not a surprise. If this becomes a desk-reject reason, the fallback venues are workshop and archival software-engineering tracks that accept participant-free evaluation (SE4All, CALE-AI already), not workarounds.
