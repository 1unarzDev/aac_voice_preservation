The situation. A person who uses AAC picks a few keywords. They may also pick a small meaning button (a chip, such as [NOT]) or a plain word that does the same job. A writing assistant turns the selection into a sentence, and the sentence is spoken as the client's message. We are asking you, as a clinician, to judge the sentences and the kinds of change. We are not asking about any person.

Task 1: How serious is each kind of change? (about 15 minutes)

Below are seven kinds of silent meaning change, each with two real examples from stored system output. For each kind, imagine the sentence were spoken as a client's message. Rate how serious that kind of change is, and say whether you would want a system to block or flag it. Rate the kind of change, using the examples as illustrations; if the two examples differ a lot for you, say so in the note.

Severity scale

  1. 1 Trivial: the listener would understand the same message. No practical consequence.
  2. 2 Minor: a small shift in tone or detail. Easily cleared up in conversation.
  3. 3 Moderate: a noticeably different message. Likely confusion or an awkward exchange, usually repairable.
  4. 4 Serious: a clearly wrong or misleading message. Could lead to a wrong action, or damage trust or the client's standing.
  5. 5 Critical: could cause real harm (health, safety, money, legal), or makes the client say the opposite of what they mean.

Task 2: Is each sentence acceptable? (about 40 minutes)

Each card shows what the person selected and the sentence a system wrote. You do not know which system or setting produced it. Answer two questions for each, and add a note if you like.

  • (a) Does it say what the selected words mean? Yes if it keeps everything the selection means and adds nothing that matters. Partly if it keeps some of it, or adds or shifts something. No if the meaning is lost or contradicted.
  • (b) Would it be acceptable to speak this as the client's message? Yes as it stands; Yes, with an edit if you would accept it after a small fix; No.

Judge the sentence against the selection only. Do not guess what the person might have wanted beyond it. Some selections are very short, and some sentences are awkward; rate what is on the page. Some inputs appear more than once with different sentences. Rate each card on its own.
